## 18 – Before We Named It

There was a time when the forest needed no signs reminding the trees to grow together. The river did not study how to flow, nor did the birds gather to debate the meaning of song. Each belonged to the rhythm that first gave it breath. Only when the path became overgrown did travelers begin placing stones to mark the way home. The markers were helpful, yet they also whispered of something forgotten. So it is with the heart. When we wander far from the spring, we become occupied with remembering what once arose as naturally as morning light upon the hills. Perhaps our finest words are often gentle echoes of a music that was already playing long before we thought to describe it.

I have noticed that the Beloved rarely praised those who performed love for the sake of being seen. Instead, He seemed drawn to hearts that had become so rooted in mercy they scarcely noticed themselves being merciful. Like bread shared among friends, like water offered to the weary, kindness simply flowed because it could do nothing else. It may be that the deepest holiness is almost unconscious. The branch does not struggle to convince the vine it belongs. It simply bears fruit when the season arrives. As we return again and again to the quiet Presence within, compassion no longer feels like a duty we remember, but like our own forgotten nature awakening. Then love becomes as unforced as breathing, and goodness slips through our hands as quietly as sunlight through the leaves.

> *Awaken in me the love that needs no name.*
