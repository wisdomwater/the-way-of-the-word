## 7 – Walking Behind

In autumn I once followed an old guide through a forest where bamboo leaned over the path and the fallen leaves softened every footstep. He never hurried ahead, though he knew the way better than any of us. From time to time he would stop to let the slowest traveler catch up, smiling as though arriving together mattered more than arriving first. The stream beside the trail did much the same. It did not strive to climb the hillside or announce its importance. It simply sought the lowest places, and because of this, every root along its banks found water. I have wondered whether fullness is less like standing upon the highest peak and more like becoming a valley where others may rest.

As the years have gathered around me, I have found myself returning often to the image of the Shepherd walking behind the flock at day's end. The strongest lambs need little guidance, but the weary and wounded linger near the rear, and so the gentle footsteps remain there too. Love seems untroubled by the desire to be first. It is content to carry, to wait, to search until every wandering heart has been found. Perhaps this is why the Beloved feels so near in our smallest moments. When we loosen our grip on becoming important, we begin to notice the quiet joy of belonging. In forgetting ourselves for a little while, we discover that we have never been forgotten.

> *Teach me the way behind.*
