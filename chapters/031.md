## 31 – No One Is a Demon

When two armies face one another across a valley, banners may give them different names, but beneath the cloth are human faces. Somewhere, perhaps, a mother waits for each soldier. Somewhere there is a table where his absence will be noticed. The Sage remembers this when others forget. He does not romanticize the sword, even when necessity places it near his hand. Violence may sometimes seem unavoidable, yet it never becomes beautiful. There are no bright victories where blood has darkened the earth. When conflict ends, the wise do not dance upon the field. They walk among what remains with lowered eyes. Perhaps this is why peace is more than the absence of fighting. Peace begins when we refuse to turn another human being into a monster, when even across the deepest division we remember the mysterious life breathing within the one we have learned to call enemy.

On a hillside in Galilee, the Friend asked something almost unbearable of those who gathered around him: to let love cross the boundary drawn around the enemy. Not to pretend that cruelty is harmless, nor to call injustice good, but to refuse the deeper violence of denying another person's humanity. Later, even beneath accusation and wounds, compassion remained. I have wondered whether this is among the narrowest paths the heart can walk. It is easy to mourn those we love. It is another thing to grieve what hatred does to the one who hates us. Yet perhaps the Beloved sees every battlefield as a family tragedy. Every wound belongs somewhere within the great household of creation. When we begin to see with such eyes, victory itself becomes a sorrowful word. We still resist what destroys life, but we do so without surrendering love.

> *Teach me to see no one as beyond compassion.*
