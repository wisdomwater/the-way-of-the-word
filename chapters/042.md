## 42 – The Fullness of Solitude

Before the forest becomes a thousand trees, there is a seed. Before the river divides around stones and spreads through the valley, there is a hidden spring. From simplicity comes difference, and from difference comes the endless dance of the ten thousand things: light and shadow, mountain and hollow, giving and receiving, movement and rest. The Sage does not try to erase these opposites. She allows them to meet within her as two banks make room for a river. Even solitude becomes part of this harmony. At first, aloneness can feel like an empty house after everyone has gone. We hurry to fill it with voices. Yet if we remain, the silence slowly changes. Wind moves through the bamboo. A bird calls from somewhere beyond sight. The boundary around the self grows softer, and what seemed like isolation becomes a doorway into belonging.

The Friend often withdrew to lonely places. Before dawn, while others slept, he walked into the hills and allowed silence to receive him. Perhaps solitude was not an escape from love, but a return to the deep well from which love could flow. There is an aloneness that closes the heart, and another that opens it until the whole world seems strangely near. The Desert Fathers discovered this beneath immense skies. The hermit in his cell could carry cities within his prayer. And perhaps we discover it too when we stop fleeing the quiet. Beneath our roles and relationships, beneath every voice that tells us who we are, there remains the simple mystery of being. Alone before the Beloved, we may discover that we are not separate after all. The silence is inhabited. The empty room is vast. Somehow, in having no one beside us, we find ourselves among everything.

> *Meet me in solitude until I discover the whole.*
