## 58 – A Light That Does Not Blind

The bamboo does not become straight because someone pulls it upward. It rises according to the life within it, bending when the wind comes and returning when the wind has passed. The Sage understands how easily even noble intentions can become heavy hands. We want others to be happy, wise, generous, pure—and before long we are tugging at their branches, trying to make them grow according to our design. Yet goodness forced from outside often learns only how to hide. So she chooses another way. Her life becomes an invitation rather than a command. She speaks clearly without cutting, stands firmly without becoming rigid, shines without demanding that anyone look at her. Like a lantern beside a dark path, she does not drag travelers toward the road. She simply remains lit, trusting that those who need the light will recognize it.

The Friend seemed to carry this kind of radiance. People came near him who had learned to avoid the righteous: tax collectors, wounded women, children, doubters, those whose lives had wandered far from respectable paths. Something in his holiness made room rather than closing it. He could speak a piercing truth and somehow leave the person before him more visible, not less. Perhaps this is because love does not need to dominate what it hopes to transform. The Beloved stands at the door and knocks; the door is not broken down. Grace invites, waits, accompanies. I have come to wonder whether the most faithful life may be one that makes goodness beautiful without making it compulsory. We become a small lamp, perhaps, offering enough light for another person's next step while trusting the hidden Presence already at work within them. The sun rises without shouting, and still the flowers turn toward it.

> *Let my life shine without casting a shadow over another.*
