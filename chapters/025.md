## 25 – Before the First Morning

Before the first mountain lifted its face toward the sky, before rivers learned their winding paths toward the sea, there was the nameless stillness. No bird had yet broken the silence. No moon had silvered the bamboo leaves. There was no here or there, no before or after—only the deep serenity from which all things would someday arise. We give it names because the heart longs to speak of what it loves, yet every name is only a small cup lowered into an immeasurable sea. The Way cannot be contained by the word we give it. It moves through stone and cedar, through cloud and sparrow, through the breath entering our lungs at this very moment. Everything seems to emerge from this hidden Source, flourish for its season, and then quietly return, like autumn leaves settling again into the earth that first nourished them.

Sometimes, in the silence before prayer becomes words, I glimpse something of the same mystery. The Beloved seems older than every name by which I have called Him, nearer than the breath with which I call. Before the lilies opened their first petals, before fishermen drew their first nets through Galilee, before anyone whispered *God*, the Holy was already here. Perhaps creation is not something that happened once in some unreachable beginning. Perhaps it is still pouring from that hidden depth: the universe receiving itself moment by moment, earth receiving the heavens, and we receiving our lives from the earth beneath our feet. The Friend invited us to become like children, and perhaps this is part of their wisdom. They receive before they explain. They belong before they understand. And beneath everything we think we know, the ancient Mystery continues giving itself away.

> *Beloved, return me to the Source from which all things arise.*
