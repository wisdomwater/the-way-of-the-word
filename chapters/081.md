## 81 – The Open Hand

At the end of a long journey, the traveler discovers how little he needed to carry. The arguments that once seemed urgent have grown quiet. The need to be understood has softened. Even words, those faithful companions, begin to fall away like leaves at the end of autumn. The Sage no longer needs to win anyone to his side. He speaks when speech is useful and becomes silent when silence is truer. His hands, once busy gathering, have opened as well. What passes through them is given freely—bread, time, wisdom, forgiveness—and somehow he does not become poorer. The river loses nothing by watering the valley. The candle is not diminished when another wick receives its flame. Perhaps this is the final simplicity: nothing to defend, nothing to prove, nothing to possess. Only a life becoming gift, quietly nourishing whatever it touches without asking that anything bear its name.

And so I think again of the Friend at the table, taking bread into his hands. He blessed it, broke it, and gave it away. Perhaps the whole path is hidden in that small gesture. Receive. Give thanks. Be broken open. Give yourself away. The Beloved does not force the heart into transformation. Love stands at the door without breaking it down, leads without domination, nourishes like rain falling quietly through the night. After all these miles through bamboo forests and along Galilean shores, I find myself with fewer conclusions than when I began. Yet perhaps that too is grace. The Way was never something to possess. God was never an answer to win. There is only this invitation to become open—to love without grasping, serve without needing recognition, and trust the hidden Life carrying all things home. Then words end. The path continues. And somewhere ahead, the river disappears into the sea.

> *Let my life become an open hand.*
