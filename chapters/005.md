## 5 – The Quiet Center

The old blacksmith in the mountain village taught me more by tending his fire than by answering my questions. Again and again he worked the bellows, and each patient breath awakened the coals without ever becoming the flame itself. The bellows remained hollow, and because it remained hollow, warmth poured from it. I have watched the same mystery in the valley where cool air descends each evening, making room for the day's heat to rise. The empty places do not resist what passes through them. They simply welcome. Perhaps the heart is fashioned for such openness. When it no longer insists upon standing above one person or below another, it begins to breathe with the quiet rhythm of creation itself.

Many years later I sat beside a charcoal fire near the shore while bread warmed on smooth stones and fishermen mended their torn nets. The Beloved looked upon weathered faces, eager faces, doubtful faces, and wounded faces with the same unhurried gaze. No one needed to become someone else before drawing near. The sea received every river without asking where it had wandered, and the morning light rested upon every boat alike. I have wondered if mercy is something like this: a spaciousness that neither excuses nor condemns, but patiently makes room for every soul to awaken. The closer we remain to that hidden center, the less we feel compelled to measure ourselves against another, and the more quietly love finds its way through us.

> *Keep me near the quiet center.*
