## 71 – The Healing of Unknowing

There is a peculiar sickness in believing the cup is already full. Nothing more can be poured into it. The Sage notices this illness first within herself: the quick judgment, the confident answer, the quiet assumption that she already understands the person standing before her. So she begins a gentle medicine of emptying. *Perhaps I do not know.* The words create space. Suddenly the stranger becomes mysterious again. The mountain she has climbed a hundred times reveals a path she never noticed. Even her own heart becomes unfamiliar country. Not-knowing is not ignorance cherished for its own sake. It is the clear-eyed humility of standing before a universe too vast to fit inside our thoughts. The mind remains useful, but no longer sits upon a throne. It becomes a bowl held beneath the rain—open, receptive, grateful for whatever arrives.

I think of the old cloud that gathered around the mountain, where the path toward God disappeared into darkness. The contemplatives learned to enter that cloud without demanding that it become clear. Even the Friend sometimes answered questions with questions, opening doors rather than closing them with certainty. Perhaps faith ripens in this direction. We begin by gathering answers, and someday discover that the Beloved is larger than every answer we have gathered. Then something within us relaxes. We no longer need to explain every sorrow, judge every soul, or trace the boundaries of divine mercy. We can simply remain near. The strange medicine begins to work: certainty softens into wonder, judgment into curiosity, fear into trust. And what once felt like losing our knowledge becomes a deeper kind of wholeness—the freedom to stand before Mystery with empty hands and an open heart.

> *Heal me of certainty and keep me open to wonder.*
