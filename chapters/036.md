## 36 – The Hidden Work

There are things the river knows that the hammer does not. Water does not argue with stone. It touches the same hard surface again and again, sometimes for a thousand years, until a valley appears where none had been. The Sage has learned to trust such quiet movements. She does not tear open the flower to hurry its blooming or dig beneath the soil each morning to see whether the seed has changed. Some things must be allowed to reach their fullness before they can pass away. Some knots loosen only after we stop pulling them tighter. The soft persists where the hard exhausts itself; the slow arrives where haste loses its way. Much of what matters seems to happen beyond our sight—in roots, in darkness, in silence. Perhaps wisdom is learning to cooperate with this hidden rhythm, doing what is ours and leaving the deeper work unnamed.

The Gardener once spoke of seed scattered upon the earth, growing while the farmer slept and rose, though he could not say how. I have always loved that small mystery. The kingdom seems to flourish in hidden places: yeast buried in flour, treasure beneath a field, life enclosed within a grain that disappears into darkness. Even the great morning of resurrection began unseen, behind a stone, before anyone arrived at the garden. We often want to understand how the Beloved is changing us. We examine ourselves for progress, pull at old wounds, measure our faith, and wonder why transformation comes so slowly. But perhaps grace prefers roots to spectacle. Something may already be softening beneath the surface. Our task is not always to understand the work. Sometimes it is simply to remain in the soil, receive the rain, and trust what quietly grows toward the light.

> *Teach me to trust Your hidden work.*
