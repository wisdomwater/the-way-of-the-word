## 63 – The Greatness of Small Things

A mountain path is crossed one stone at a time. The great cedar begins as something that could rest unnoticed in the palm. Even the river that cuts through cliffs gathers itself first from droplets, springs, and small streams slipping quietly downhill. The Sage does not despise beginnings because they are small. She notices the loose thread before the cloth unravels, tends the young shoot before weeds surround it, and gives herself to the next step without demanding to see the summit. When difficulty comes, she does not immediately turn away in search of easier ground. She stops. She listens. She lets the obstacle become part of the path. Perhaps much of our suffering comes from resisting the small discomfort before us while imagining the enormous struggle ahead. Yet life rarely asks us to climb the whole mountain at once. Usually it offers one stone, then another.

The Friend seemed to cherish small things that others overlooked. A cup of water. A widow's coins. A little bread placed into willing hands. A mustard seed disappearing into the soil. He spoke as though the kingdom itself preferred to arrive unnoticed, hidden within ordinary acts of faithfulness. Perhaps we miss much of the Beloved because we keep waiting for something grand. We imagine holiness as a distant summit while love waits in the dishes beside the sink, the person who needs listening, the apology we have postponed, the quiet task that belongs to this hour. None of these things appears great. Yet a life is made from such moments, as a cathedral is raised from stones no single hand could lift all at once. We need not reach for greatness. We can simply become present to what love asks now and allow eternity to gather itself from our small yes.

> *Teach me to meet the great Way in the smallest step.*
