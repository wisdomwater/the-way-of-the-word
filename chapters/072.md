## 72 – Before the Temple

Long before there was a temple, there was the mountain. Before incense curled toward painted ceilings, mist rose from the river at dawn. A child needed no instruction to become silent beneath a sky crowded with stars. Awe came first. The Sage remembers this and is careful not to stand between another person and the mystery. He points, then lowers his hand. He speaks, then leaves enough silence for the listener to hear something deeper than his words. There is a kind of teaching that makes disciples dependent upon the teacher, and another that quietly returns them to their own seeing. The Sage prefers the second. He does not want people forever carrying his lantern. He hopes their eyes will adjust to the dark. Perhaps the truest guide eventually becomes unnecessary, like a finger pointing toward the moon, forgotten once the moon itself fills the sky.

The Friend seemed to do something similar. He spoke in stories that refused to become tidy explanations—seeds, lamps, vineyards, lost coins, birds crossing the sky. Again and again, the listener was left with something to ponder rather than merely memorize. And when people searched for the Holy in distant places, he drew their attention toward a kingdom already near, within and among them. Perhaps religion is most beautiful when it protects this wonder without trying to possess it. A chapel can become a doorway, a prayer can become a window, a teacher can accompany us toward the threshold. But the Beloved waits beyond every form and also within it, nearer than any authority can stand. Eventually even our finest teachings must become quiet. We step through the doorway ourselves and discover that there was nothing new to learn—only a Presence we had somehow forgotten how to notice.

> *Restore the wonder that existed before my answers.*
