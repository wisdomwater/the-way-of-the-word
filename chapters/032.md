## 32 – Where the Rivers Return

Stand beside the sea long enough and you may begin to wonder where one river ends and another begins. Mountain streams, hidden springs, rainwater slipping from bamboo leaves—all eventually surrender their names to something larger. So it may be with the Way. We divide the world because names help us walk through it: river and ocean, earth and sky, this and that, mine and yours. These distinctions are useful, but perhaps they are not the deepest truth. The smallest grain of sand contains mysteries beyond our seeing, while the night above it opens into distances beyond imagining. The Sage holds names lightly. She builds what is needed, uses what is useful, and knows when the scaffolding has served its purpose. Forms may guide us toward the mystery, but wisdom remembers that the mystery cannot be contained by the forms. Every cup has an edge. The sea does not.

I have felt this most strongly in quiet sanctuaries after everyone has gone home. The candles remain, the wooden cross rests in shadow, the last notes of prayer have disappeared into silence. These things are precious because they point beyond themselves. Even our finest words for the Beloved are little boats upon an immeasurable ocean. The Friend once spoke of a law written not upon stone but within the heart, as though the deepest sanctuary might finally be found where no walls are needed. Perhaps paradise begins there—not when everyone is forced into the same shape, but when love rises from within as naturally as water seeks the sea. Churches, teachings, rituals, and names may accompany us faithfully along the riverbank. Yet the current keeps drawing us onward, toward the One from whom all things come and into whom all things quietly return.

> *Carry me like a river into Your boundless sea.*
