## 41 – The Wisdom That Looks Like Foolishness

The mountain path sometimes descends when the summit seems near. It disappears beneath cedar shadows, doubles back upon itself, and leads the traveler away from the peak he thought he was approaching. Someone watching from the valley might laugh and say he has lost his way. Yet the mountain knows paths the eye cannot see. So it is with the deeper Way. Strength may arrive dressed as softness. Clarity may wait inside bewilderment. The finest bowl may bear the marks of the potter’s fingers, while the oldest tree twists rather than standing straight. The Sage has learned not to judge too quickly by appearances. He does not fear looking foolish when wisdom asks him to become simple. He walks into darkness when the path leads there, trusting that what nourishes the roots is often hidden from the sun. The unseen Way completes what spectacle cannot.

Perhaps this is why the Carpenter’s life remains so strange. A king appears without a palace. The blessed are the poor, the grieving, the meek. Greatness kneels with a towel. Enemies are answered with love. A child becomes the image of wisdom, and the road toward life passes through a cross. None of this looks like the way we would design salvation. Even those nearest to him often seemed bewildered by the path. Yet perhaps divine wisdom has always appeared foolish to the part of us that measures everything by victory, certainty, and control. The Beloved keeps inviting us down roads that seem to lead backward—toward surrender, forgiveness, hiddenness, and trust. We may not always recognize the light while walking through it. Sometimes only later do we discover that the darkness itself had been quietly nourishing our sight.

> *Give me courage to follow the wisdom I cannot yet understand.*
