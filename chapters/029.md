## 29 – At the Center of the Circle

There is a temptation, when we love the garden, to pull at every shoot so that it might grow faster. We straighten the young tree, move the stones, divert the stream, prune what seems untidy—and sometimes discover that our improvements have wounded what was already learning how to flourish. The Sage approaches the world differently. She bows before it as something sacred, not something placed into her hands for mastery. She knows there are mornings for climbing the mountain and afternoons for sitting beneath its shade. There are seasons when strength rises easily and others when even a few steps feel heavy. She does not demand summer from winter. She does not shame the moon for becoming dark. Standing quietly at the center, she allows each thing its season, participating without possessing, tending without trying to make the whole world obey her hands.

The Gardener once spoke of wheat and weeds growing together in the same field. The servants were eager to pull away what did not belong, but patience saw what urgency could not: roots had become entangled beneath the soil. To tear out one might wound the other. There is a mercy in knowing when not to interfere. The Beloved did not seem indifferent to suffering; hands reached toward the sick, the hungry were fed, tables were overturned when love required it. Yet even these movements arose from somewhere deeper than the hunger to control. Perhaps this is the center we are invited to inhabit—a still place from which action can emerge without grasping. We mend what love places before us, rest when evening comes, and leave the great unfolding in hands larger than our own. The world is not ours to possess. It is ours to cherish.

> *Beloved, keep me near the still center of love.*
