## 8 – Like Water

There is a stream that winds through the valley below the monastery. I have watched it in every season. In spring it dances over smooth stones with laughter. In summer it slips quietly beneath the shade of willow and bamboo. When autumn fills it with fallen leaves, it carries them gently without complaint. It never argues with the shape of the land. It does not seek the mountain peak or envy the clouds above. It is content to find the lowest places, and there it becomes a drink for the thirsty, a mirror for the sky, and a home for countless small lives. Perhaps the deepest strength has never been found in rising above others, but in drawing near enough to refresh them.

One evening I sat beside the sea while fishermen untangled their nets in the fading light. Nearby, a small child knelt at the water's edge, watching each wave erase the footprints she had carefully made in the sand. She laughed every time they disappeared, then began again. The Friend seemed very much like the sea that evening—never hurried, never grasping, always making room for another beginning. I have found that the soul grows most peacefully when it no longer strains to become remarkable. A loaf shared around a worn table, a listening ear, a hand resting gently upon another's shoulder—these quiet offerings flow through the world like living water. They ask for no applause, yet they leave everything greener than they found it.

> *Let me flow with quiet love.*
