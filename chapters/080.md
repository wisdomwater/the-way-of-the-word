## 80 – The Kingdom Close to Home

There is a village where morning begins with the sound of a broom against the doorstep. Someone kneads bread. Someone mends a fence. In a nearby garden, beans climb their poles while children chase one another between the rows. Nothing here would impress a traveler searching for wonders. Yet the Sage senses a hidden richness in such ordinary life. The tools fit the hands that use them. Meals taste good because hunger has preceded them. Neighbors know one another’s names. Beyond the hills there may be splendid cities, distant markets, and roads leading toward a thousand experiences, but the heart does not ache to be elsewhere. Perhaps simplicity is not the rejection of invention or travel, but freedom from the belief that life is always happening somewhere else. The kettle sings. Evening gathers around familiar faces. And suddenly the small place where we stand becomes spacious enough.

I imagine many years in Nazareth passing this way, almost entirely unrecorded. Wood shavings on a workshop floor. Bread cooling near a window. Familiar voices in the street. Sabbath lamps glowing as evening settled over the village. Before crowds gathered beside the lake, the Friend seems to have spent most of his life inside this quiet holiness of ordinary things. Perhaps the kingdom was already there. We are tempted to search for God in distant experiences, dramatic awakenings, and extraordinary places, while the Beloved waits beside the kitchen table. There is holiness in preparing a meal, tending a garden, laughing with family, repairing what has broken, listening to a neighbor tell the same story again. None of this asks us to make our world small. It asks us to become present enough to discover how large the nearby world has always been.

> *Teach me to find Your kingdom close to home.*
