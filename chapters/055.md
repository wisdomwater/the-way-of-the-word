## 55 – The Strength of a Child

A newborn seems almost impossibly fragile. Its limbs are soft, its body small, yet life moves through it with an ancient intensity. Tiny fingers close around one of ours and hold with surprising strength. Breath becomes a cry without hesitation. Hunger comes, milk is received, sleep arrives, and each is entered completely before being released. The child has not yet learned to stand outside life and measure it. There is no reputation to protect, no future harvest to demand from the seed just planted. The Sage returns toward something of this original vitality. He does not become childish; he becomes unclenched. Experiences come and go like clouds across the mountain, and he does not exhaust himself trying to hold them in place. Expectation loosens, disappointment loses its teeth, and something within him remains young—not untouched by sorrow, but still able to meet the morning without demanding what it must become.

Perhaps this is why the Friend placed a child in the midst of grown people discussing greatness. The small one had accomplished nothing, possessed no rank, and brought no argument to defend. Yet somehow this became an image of the kingdom. I wonder whether the Beloved is forever inviting us back toward such receptivity. We have accumulated so many expectations about how life should unfold, how prayer should feel, what our labor should produce, even who we should have become by now. These expectations can make the soul old before the body is. But grace comes like breath to an infant—received before it is understood. We offer our work without demanding its fruit. We love without possessing the outcome. We allow evening to close the day that morning opened. And perhaps the spirit remains young wherever it can still receive life as gift.

> *Make my heart young enough to receive without grasping.*
