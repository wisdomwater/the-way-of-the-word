## 76 – The Tenderness of Life

In spring, the newest branches bend beneath the slightest rain. Their bark is thin, their leaves almost translucent, yet within that tenderness life is rising with astonishing strength. The dead branch is different. It holds its shape perfectly. It refuses the wind, and because it cannot bend, the storm eventually breaks it. The Sage has watched enough seasons to know that hardness is not always strength. Life remains supple. It adjusts, receives, yields, and continues. Perhaps the heart is much the same. We become brittle when every opinion must be defended, every wound guarded, every change resisted. We call this firmness, but sometimes it is only fear that has forgotten how to move. The Way invites us toward another strength—the strength of water, young branches, open hands. To remain tender in a hard world is not weakness. It may be one of the clearest signs that life still moves within us.

The Friend seemed remarkably difficult to harden. Criticism did not make him cruel. Betrayal did not teach him contempt. He could stand firmly before those who burdened others and then become gentle enough for children to climb into his arms. Even after the nails, the wounds remained open rather than becoming weapons. I have often wondered about that image: the risen one still bearing scars, yet no longer bound by the violence that made them. Perhaps this is what resurrection looks like within the heart. We do not become untouched by what has happened to us. We become soft again without denying the wound. The Beloved breathes life into places that have grown rigid with fear, loosening what we thought must remain clenched forever. And slowly we discover that holiness may not be the strength to remain unmoved. It may be the grace to bend without breaking, to be wounded without becoming hard, and to keep choosing love.

> *Keep my heart tender enough for life to move through it.*
