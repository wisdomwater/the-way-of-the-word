## 17 – The Quiet Gardener

The finest gardeners I have known rarely spoke about their gardens. Before sunrise they loosened the soil, carried water to thirsty roots, and quietly pulled the weeds before anyone noticed they had appeared. By midsummer, the trees bent low with fruit and the flowers greeted every passerby, yet no sign bore the gardener's name. Children wandered the paths believing the orchard had simply learned to flourish on its own. The old man would smile at this and continue his work without correction. The rain never announces itself before it falls. The valley never asks to be thanked for receiving the river. Perhaps the truest gifts become almost invisible, leaving behind not admiration for the giver, but gratitude that life itself has quietly blossomed.

The Carpenter often moved through the world with that same hidden gentleness. He knelt more than He stood above others. He asked questions that awakened sleeping hearts rather than offering answers that demanded applause. Those who walked beside Him gradually found themselves becoming more patient, more merciful, more alive, until one day they discovered that compassion had become as natural as breathing. It may be that this is how the Beloved continues His work even now—not by overpowering the soul, but by nurturing what has always rested there in quiet seed. Love does not seek to make us dependent upon itself. It delights when we begin to recognize its own life flowering within us. Then every kindness feels strangely familiar, and we smile with quiet wonder, believing we have finally learned to love, never realizing that Love has been gently growing us all along.

> *Let my life quietly reveal Your love.*
