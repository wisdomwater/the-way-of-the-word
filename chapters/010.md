## 10 – Returning to the Beginning

At first light I sometimes watch the mist drift across the valley until the hills and the sky can no longer be told apart. A newborn fawn steps uncertainly through the tall grass, bending with every uneven stone instead of resisting it. The bamboo yields to the morning wind without losing its rootedness, and the stream finds its way around every obstacle without forgetting the sea toward which it quietly flows. The world seems to remember something we often forget: life is strongest when it is supple, clearest when it is unhurried, and most whole when it no longer strains to become something other than it already is. Perhaps the oldest wisdom is not hidden in distant places, but waiting beneath the layers we have gathered around ourselves.

I once saw the Gardener kneel beside a young vine that had grown tangled among the rocks. He did not pull it upward by force. With patient hands he loosened the branches, lifted them gently toward the light, and trusted the living vine to do what it had always longed to do. I have carried that image through many seasons. The Beloved does not hurry the blossoming of the soul. Bread rises in silence. Seeds awaken beneath unseen soil. Children learn trust by resting in welcoming arms before they ever understand the world around them. It may be that holiness is less a matter of mastering life than of yielding to the quiet Love that has been shaping us from the beginning, asking only that we remain open enough to grow.

> *Return me to quiet oneness.*
