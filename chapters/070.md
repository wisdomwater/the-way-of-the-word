## 70 – Older Than Words

The river knew how to descend before anyone named humility. Bamboo bent beneath the wind before a teacher spoke of yielding. Mothers gathered children to their breasts before compassion was written in a book. The deepest wisdom seems to belong to this ancient country, older than our explanations of it. The Sage does not despise the intellect; it is a fine lantern for walking certain roads. But there are places where the lantern cannot illuminate what the heart already knows. We can study water for years and still remain thirsty beside the stream. We can describe the path perfectly and never place our feet upon it. Perhaps the Way becomes difficult only when we try to possess it as an idea. When the mind finally grows tired of grasping, something simpler appears beneath it: breathe, soften, listen, receive. What is oldest in us seems to recognize what is older than the world.

The Friend often offered teachings simple enough for a child to carry: forgive, love, give, follow, remain. Yet each opens into depths no lifetime could exhaust. We can build elaborate towers of thought around love while avoiding the person before us who needs it. We can speak beautifully of prayer while never becoming quiet enough to pray. Perhaps this is why the Beloved continually draws us from the head into the hidden chamber of the heart. Not because thought is an enemy, but because mystery must finally become lived. The kingdom is like bread: understanding its ingredients cannot nourish us until we eat. And when we turn inward, beneath our arguments and carefully constructed selves, we may discover a quiet recognition already waiting there. The heart knows the fragrance of home. It hears something ancient in the voice of love and whispers, without knowing how it knows: *Yes. This way.*

> *Lead me beneath my thoughts into the wisdom of the heart.*
