## 74 – The Open Hand

Autumn does not argue with the falling leaf. The tree releases what summer gave it, and the wind carries gold and crimson across the path. Nothing in the forest remains as it was, yet nothing seems offended by the changing. The Sage watches and slowly opens his hands. He knows how much suffering is born from asking a passing thing not to pass—a season, a face, a body, a life we have carefully arranged. Even tomorrow becomes something we try to seize before it arrives. But the future is wood beneath another Carpenter’s hands. When we grasp for the tools, trying to carve every outcome according to our fear, we often wound ourselves upon the blade. Perhaps freedom begins when we stop demanding permanence from a world made of seasons. We receive what comes with gratitude, cherish it while it remains, and when its hour arrives, practice the difficult tenderness of release.

The Carpenter of Galilee knew wood, and perhaps this is why I sometimes imagine the Beloved’s providence as a workbench hidden from our sight. We see only the rough timber of the present moment and cannot yet imagine what shape may emerge. Our instinct is to reach across the bench, seize the tools, and help the future become what we think it should be. Yet the Friend invited his companions into another posture: receive today’s bread; let tomorrow carry its own concerns; place even life itself into hands deeper than your own. This is not indifference to the future. Seeds are still planted, wounds tended, journeys prepared. But beneath such care there can be surrender. Death itself becomes the final unclenching, the last thing we cannot manage into obedience. Perhaps when we stop trying to possess tomorrow, we discover how completely this one unrepeatable day can be loved.

> *Open my hands to receive today and release tomorrow.*
