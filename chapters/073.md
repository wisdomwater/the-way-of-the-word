## 73 – Nothing Slips Away

A spider’s web can be almost invisible in the morning light. Only when dew gathers upon its threads do we notice how far it reaches—one silver line joined to another, spanning spaces that moments before seemed empty. The Sage has come to trust that life may be held by something just as quiet. The Way does not hurry through the world arranging every leaf. It does not compete with the river or shout instructions to the stars. Things unfold, seasons turn, wounds slowly close, and the hidden pattern remains larger than what the eye can follow. We make our plans because plans are useful, but perhaps reality rests upon something deeper than planning. The great net is spacious enough for freedom, wandering, even loss, yet somehow nothing falls beyond it. What appears forgotten may still belong. What seems hopelessly scattered may remain held by threads too fine for us to see.

I think of the Shepherd speaking of a sparrow falling unnoticed to the ground and yet somehow not unnoticed at all. A sheep wanders from the flock, and the Shepherd enters the hills. A coin disappears into darkness, and a lamp is lit. A son travels into a distant country, and still the road home remains open. Again and again, the Friend painted a world in which lostness was real but never final enough to exhaust love. Perhaps divine care is less like a fist holding everything tightly and more like an immeasurable net beneath creation—wide enough for our freedom, strong enough for our wandering. We may travel far. We may lose sight of the shore. We may even imagine ourselves abandoned in some distant darkness. Yet perhaps the Beloved has never lost sight of us. Beneath every falling thing, mercy has already spread its net.

> *Let me rest in the mercy from which nothing can fall.*
