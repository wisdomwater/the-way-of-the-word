## 13 – Standing on the Ground

The mountain has watched many generations climb its slopes. Some hurried upward, eager to stand where all could see them. Others stumbled downward, ashamed of losing their footing. Yet the mountain remained unmoved by either triumph or defeat. The pine did not grow taller because it was praised, nor did the stream cease its singing because no one paused to listen. The earth beneath every traveler welcomed each footstep with the same quiet patience. Perhaps this is why the old gardener smiled more often than he celebrated. He had learned that blossoms open and petals fall according to a rhythm no hand can command. To cling to the season of flowering is to fear the coming of autumn. To dread winter is to miss the sweetness already ripening in the orchard.

The Carpenter once knelt to wash dust from weary feet. The hands that could have reached for thrones instead reached for a basin. In that simple act, the illusion of climbing dissolved like morning mist beneath the sun. It may be that the heart finds its truest balance when it no longer measures itself against another, nor even against its own imagined reflection. The Beloved does not ask us to become greater than the world, but to discover that we have never been separate from the life flowing through it. When another weeps, something within us already knows the taste of those tears. When another rejoices, our own soul quietly blooms beside them. The sky does not divide its light, and Love does not keep careful accounts. Standing upon such ground, there is nowhere left to fall.

> *Root me in the ground of Love.*
