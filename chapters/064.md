## 64 – Beneath Your Feet

The giant pine begins so quietly that no one thinks to call it great. A tender green shoot pushes through the soil, easily shaded, easily bent, yet within it sleeps a tree beneath which generations may someday rest. The Sage pays attention to such beginnings. He knows that a small resentment is easier to release before it becomes a hardened grievance, that a whispered falsehood is easier to correct before a life is built around it. Yet he does not confuse attentiveness with hurry. Fruit pulled from the branch too soon remains bitter. A journey becomes exhausting when the traveler keeps staring at the distant mountain instead of the ground beneath his feet. So he begins where he is. One step. One breath. One small act of care. And when the destination seems near, he remains as patient as he was at the beginning, allowing what has ripened to fall into his hands.

The Shepherd also seemed drawn to beginnings others could barely see: mustard seeds, yeast hidden in flour, a little bread, a few fishermen beside a lake. The kingdom did not arrive fully grown. It appeared beneath ordinary feet and quietly invited people to follow. Perhaps the inner journey unfolds in the same way. We want to become holy, healed, wise, complete, and in reaching too eagerly for the finished self, we can become harsh toward the person who is still growing. But the Beloved does not seem to force the blossom open. Grace reminds us, patiently, of something older than all our striving—the image already breathed into us, the child beneath our defenses, the belovedness that preceded every achievement. Perhaps transformation is less about manufacturing a new self than releasing what obscures the deepest one. We unlearn our grasping, loosen our fear, and discover that the road home has always begun beneath our feet.

> *Teach me to begin here and trust what You are growing.*
