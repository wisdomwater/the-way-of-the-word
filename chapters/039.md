## 39 – As Common as a Stone

After rain, the mountain seems to breathe. Clouds lift from the pines, water gathers in hollows, and small creatures emerge from places no eye had noticed. Nothing stands alone. The moss needs the stone, the cedar drinks from the dark earth, the stream carries what the mountain releases, and the valley receives it all. The Sage sees this belonging and becomes gentle with the parts because she has glimpsed the whole. She knows how easily a grasping hand can disturb what took centuries to form. So she walks lightly. She does not need to glitter like jade among ordinary stones. She would rather become simple enough to belong—to be shaped by weather, softened by water, warmed by sunlight, useful without demanding attention. Perhaps humility is remembering that we are not standing above the great web of life. We are threads within it, held by what we also help to hold.

The Shepherd seemed at home among common things: dust beneath bare feet, fishing boats pulled onto shore, grain between his fingers, bread and wine upon a table. He spoke of sparrows as though heaven noticed their falling and of wildflowers as though their brief beauty mattered to the One who clothed them. Nothing was merely background. The earth itself seemed alive with the generosity of the Beloved. Perhaps this is why love for God eventually widens into tenderness for everything that breathes. The heart begins to see kinship where it once saw objects. Soil becomes gift. Water becomes blessing. The stranger becomes neighbor. Even our own small life loses its need to be exceptional. We need not shine above creation. It may be enough to become like a stone beside the path—ordinary, weathered, resting where we have been placed, quietly warmed by the same sun that rises upon all.

> *Make me humble enough to belong to everything You love.*
