## 27 – Nothing Is Wasted

The traveler who must know every turning before beginning may never leave the village gate. But another steps onto the road with a small pack and an open heart, allowing the mountain to reveal itself one bend at a time. The artist waits before the blank page until some hidden movement of the brush begins. The wise observer looks again at what everyone believes has already been understood. There is a spaciousness in such living. Nothing is dismissed too quickly. No person is reduced to the worst thing they have done. No circumstance is declared useless simply because it was unwanted. The Sage gathers even broken pieces from the floor, wondering what they might yet become. Fallen leaves feed the roots. Crooked branches shelter birds. A wrong turning may reveal a stream we would otherwise never have seen. In the hands of the Way, perhaps very little needs to be wasted.

The Shepherd seemed to wander with this same openness. He stopped for the person others hurried past, ate with those respectable tables would not welcome, and saw possibilities hidden beneath names like sinner, foreigner, failure, and fool. Even those who opposed him were not beyond the reach of his gaze. Perhaps this is the great secret: we belong to one another more deeply than our judgments allow. The wounded may teach the healer tenderness. The lost may awaken the searcher. The person whose life seems tangled may become the very place where another learns patience and mercy. And tomorrow their places may be reversed. The Gardener throws nothing away too quickly. What appears dead may still nourish the soil. What appears hopeless may be waiting beneath the earth. Perhaps embodying the light means learning to look upon every person and every moment with the quiet question: *What might love make of this?*

> *Teach me to see where nothing is wasted.*
