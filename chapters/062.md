## 62 – The Refuge at the Center

At the center of the village stands a well. The careful farmer drinks from it, and so does the wanderer who has squandered everything and returned with dust upon his clothes. The water does not ask which hands are worthy of the cup. The Sage treasures the Way for this reason. It cannot be purchased with eloquence, earned by reputation, or reserved for those whose lives have remained untangled. It is treasure for the wise and refuge for the foolish, nearer than all the distinctions by which we separate one another. We spend much of life trying to become worthy of what has never been for sale. Yet the great river keeps flowing. The moon continues to appear in muddy water as readily as in a polished bowl. Perhaps the deepest gift cannot be achieved because it was never withheld. We do not climb toward it. We awaken to where we already stand.

The Friend seemed especially fond of revealing treasure in unlikely places. A wandering son discovered home after wasting his inheritance. A tax collector climbed down from a tree and found himself welcomed at the table. A thief, with almost nothing left to offer, heard words of paradise. Again and again, mercy appeared before anyone could earn it. Perhaps this is what makes grace so difficult for the calculating mind and so beautiful to the weary heart. The Beloved is not merely the reward waiting for those who have traveled correctly. He is also the refuge beside the road when we discover we are lost. Even our mistakes need not become places of exile. They may become doors through which humility enters. We turn, and find that Presence has not withdrawn. We seek, and discover that we were already being sought. At the center of everything, mercy remains.

> *Be my treasure when I am faithful and my refuge when I am lost.*
