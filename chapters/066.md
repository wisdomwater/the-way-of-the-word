## 66 – The Lowest Place

Every stream descends. Rain gathers upon the high ridges, slips beneath roots and stones, and finds its way downward until at last the sea receives it all. The sea becomes vast not by climbing above the rivers, but by resting below them. The Sage learns from this. She does not need to stand taller in order to lead. She listens from the lower place, where the voices of others can reach her. She walks ahead when the path requires it, yet never so far that those behind become merely followers of her will. There is no contest to win, no position to defend. When competition falls away, a strange freedom appears. Another person's wisdom need not threaten our own. Another person's success takes nothing from us. Like the sea, we can become spacious by descending—receiving what comes without needing everything to bear our name.

The Friend once found his companions arguing about which of them was greatest. His answer was not a lecture from a throne. He knelt. A basin appeared, a towel was wrapped around his waist, and the hands they called Lord began washing road dust from their feet. Perhaps few images reveal the heart of divine power more clearly. The Beloved does not seem frightened by the lowest place. Again and again, greatness descends: into a manger, into ordinary villages, beneath the table with tired feet, finally into the darkness of a tomb. And somehow this downward path becomes the place from which life rises. Perhaps we lead most truly when others do not feel our weight upon them. We make room. We listen. We carry what is heavy when we can. And because we are no longer trying to stand above anyone, we become like the sea—low enough for many rivers to find rest within us.

> *Teach me the greatness of the lowest place.*
