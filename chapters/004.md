## 4 – The Deep Well

There is an old well beneath a grove where bamboo and olive trees seem to have agreed to grow together. Its stones have been worn smooth by generations of hands lowering empty buckets into unseen depths. Morning after morning, the water rises without announcement. Travelers drink. Children laugh as they spill it upon one another. Birds skim its surface before disappearing into the blue. Still the well does not grow anxious that it will be emptied. It keeps no account of what has been drawn from it. The deeper I peer into its quiet darkness, the less I feel I have reached the bottom. Perhaps the truest abundance is hidden where the eye cannot measure and the mind cannot name.

I have often remembered another place where thirsty souls gathered, each carrying a vessel they hoped would finally satisfy them. Some arrived with jars of certainty, others with cracked cups of sorrow, and a few with nothing at all but open hands. The Friend seemed to delight most in those empty hands. They could receive without defending what they already possessed. The spring that flowed among us was never loud, yet no one who lingered beside it departed unchanged. It may be that the Holy Mystery has always been nearer than our searching, quietly sustaining every breath while remaining wonderfully beyond every name we offer. Like living water beneath ancient stone, it waits without impatience for the thirsty heart to draw near.

> *Lead me to the hidden spring.*
