## 77 – The Bending of the Bow

Watch an archer prepare the bow. What rises must bend downward; what rests below is drawn upward. The strength of the bow comes from this meeting, from tension held in balance rather than power gathered at one end. The Sage sees the same movement throughout life. Rain leaves the swollen cloud and falls upon thirsty ground. Rivers carry mountain water into the lowlands. Fruit trees bend their branches toward whoever is hungry enough to reach. Where there is too much, life seems always searching for somewhere empty enough to receive. Yet we often reverse this movement. We gather where there is already abundance and take from places already bare. The Sage chooses the downward path instead. Because she does not clutch what passes through her hands, she discovers a peculiar wealth: there is always something left to give. The open hand is never burdened by guarding what it possesses.

The Friend once watched wealthy people place large gifts into the treasury, then noticed a poor widow offering two small coins. Somehow his gaze settled upon her. Perhaps he saw that heaven measures abundance differently. Later, bread passed through his hands on a hillside and kept moving until the hungry were fed. This seems to be the rhythm of the Beloved: fullness pouring toward emptiness, strength bending toward weakness, greatness kneeling before tired feet. Grace itself moves like the bow, drawing high things low and lifting what has fallen. And those who enter this movement need not announce their generosity. The branch does not take credit for the fruit. The spring does not engrave its name upon the cup. We simply allow what has been given to us—wealth, strength, time, tenderness—to continue its journey toward wherever there is need.

> *Let what fills my hands flow toward those whose hands are empty.*
