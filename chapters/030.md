## 30 – The Strength That Does Not Force

Strike the surface of a pond and the water answers with widening circles. Push against a branch and it bends beneath the hand, storing within itself the strength to spring back. The Sage has watched such things long enough to distrust the desire to force life into obedience. He knows that even good intentions can become harsh when clenched too tightly. So he acts when action is needed, but he does not keep pushing simply because he can. He plants the seed and leaves the earth alone. He speaks his word and allows silence to follow. He does not chase those who disagree with him down the road. Perhaps his peace comes from knowing that the river was flowing before he arrived and will continue after he is gone. He has been asked to participate in the world, not to carry it upon his shoulders.

The Carpenter carried no sword into the garden. When one was drawn in his defense, he asked that it be put away, then reached toward the wounded man. There is something astonishing in such freedom. He did not need violence to protect his truth, nor the agreement of the crowd to make his life meaningful. He could wash the feet of those who misunderstood him, remain silent before accusation, and entrust what he could not control to the Father whose rain falls upon every field. Perhaps this is the strength of the Beloved: not the power to make everything bend, but the freedom to love without needing everything to yield. When we no longer require the world to confirm who we are, our hands begin to open. And open hands can bless, heal, receive, and release without turning love into another form of control.

> *Give me strength that never needs to force.*
