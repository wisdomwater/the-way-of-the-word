## 37 – The Life That Ripens by Itself

In the valley, nothing seems to hurry, and yet nothing is neglected. Morning opens without hands pulling it across the sky. Plum blossoms unfold without instruction. The stream finds the low places, moss gathers upon old stones, and somewhere beneath the earth a seed divides itself in darkness. The Way appears to accomplish everything without the strain we so often mistake for faithfulness. The Sage learns to participate in this quiet rhythm. She sweeps the floor, carries water, prepares rice, tends what has been placed before her, and does not demand that each ordinary act become something greater. Perhaps our deepest restlessness begins when simple life no longer seems sufficient. We want another place, another possession, another version of ourselves. Yet when desire grows quiet, even briefly, the unnoticed world returns: warm tea between our hands, rain against the roof, the astonishing gift of having enough.

I imagine the Carpenter spending long years in a small village, shaping wood while seasons passed outside the doorway. Most of those days left no story behind. There were meals, neighbors, Sabbath evenings, sawdust upon the floor. Even later, he spoke of a kingdom that grew like seed while the farmer slept, of yeast quietly disappearing into dough, of birds that neither planted nor harvested and lilies that did not struggle to clothe themselves. Perhaps the life of God is nearer to this effortless unfolding than to our anxious striving. We cannot force ourselves into holiness any more than we can command dawn to arrive. We can become available. We can loosen what we clutch, attend to the small work of love, and let grace ripen according to its hidden season. Then transformation may come almost unnoticed, until one day we discover that peace has been growing within us like a garden.

> *Quiet my striving and ripen Your life in me.*
