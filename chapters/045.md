## 45 – Perfectly Itself

There is an old pine on the mountain whose trunk has been bent by decades of snow. No carpenter would call it straight. One branch reaches awkwardly toward the valley while another has long since broken away, leaving a pale scar in the bark. Yet standing beneath it, I have never wished the tree were different. Its crookedness has become its beauty. The Sage learns to see this way. She does not demand that life conform to the perfect shapes imagined in her mind. The cracked bowl still holds tea. The weathered stone still offers a place to sit. The empty room is full of silence. She works with what arrives, shaping without forcing, responding without insisting that the moment become something else. Perhaps perfection is not the absence of rough edges. Perhaps it is the quiet wholeness of a thing no longer struggling to be anything other than itself.

The Carpenter must have known the grain of wood well—the way each piece asks to be worked according to what it already is. Push against the grain and the wood splinters; learn its hidden direction and the hands begin to cooperate with something they did not create. I sometimes imagine grace working like this. The Beloved does not seem interested in carving us into identical saints. One becomes a quiet shelter, another a table where strangers gather, another something small and unnoticed that holds a door open. Even our wounds may remain visible, like scars in timber through which a deeper beauty appears. Perhaps wisdom is learning when to shape and when to step aside. We offer our hands to the work, but we do not need to control its final form. And slowly, beneath our striving, another artistry emerges—simple, unforced, and unmistakably alive.

> *Shape me gently, and teach me when to step aside.*
