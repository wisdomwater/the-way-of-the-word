## 40 – The Way of Returning

Watch the river long enough and you begin to see that its journey outward is also a journey home. Rain leaves the sea, gathers over mountains, falls into hidden springs, and wanders through valleys until at last it returns to the water from which it rose. Autumn follows summer. Fallen leaves become dark earth, and from that darkness spring rises again. The Sage does not resist this rhythm. She knows that yielding is not always defeat and returning is not always retreat. Sometimes the deepest movement forward is a turning back toward what was there before our striving began. Even emptiness has its secret fertility. The hollow flute gives music. The open doorway permits passage. The silent valley receives the river. What appears to be nothing may be the spacious womb from which something entirely new is waiting to emerge.

The Friend often spoke of returning: becoming like a child, coming home like a wandering son, losing life and finding it again. His own path descended before it rose. Bread was broken before it nourished. The seed entered the darkness before becoming wheat. And at the heart of the story stands an empty tomb—a hollow place from which life unexpectedly begins again. Perhaps this is the strange rhythm of the Beloved. We are continually invited to release what we have become so that something deeper may be born. Prayer itself sometimes feels this way. Words fall away. Images disappear. Even our sense of God may become quiet, leaving only a vast and seemingly empty darkness. Yet perhaps this emptiness is not absence. Perhaps it is the open space before creation, the silent garden before dawn, the place where returning becomes beginning.

> *Lead me through emptiness into the life that returns.*
