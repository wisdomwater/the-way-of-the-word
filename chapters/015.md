## 15 – When the Water Clears

I once knew an old traveler who never seemed to hurry, though he was seldom late. When winter laid a thin sheet of ice across the stream, he crossed with quiet attention, not because he feared the water, but because he honored it. In spring he watched snow become rivulets, and rivulets become rivers, never urging them to arrive before their time. His words were few, his laughter easy, and his hands rested lightly upon the things they held. Sitting beside him was like sitting in a deep valley where every sound softened before disappearing into stillness. If a question came, he did not seize it. He let it settle like silt in a clear pool, trusting that what belonged at the bottom would find its own resting place.

So too the Beloved seemed untouched by the urgency that filled so many hearts. Crowds pressed near, demanding answers, signs, and certainty, yet He often paused long enough to notice a child, a sparrow, or the hush that follows an evening breeze through olive branches. There is a wisdom that cannot be forced into bloom. It ripens quietly beneath the warmth of patient love. Perhaps this is why the soul grows clearer when it stops trying to manufacture its own light. We need not wrestle every moment into meaning. We need only remain available, like a still lake receiving the morning sky without effort. Then, when the time is full, the next faithful step rises from within as naturally as a seed breaking open beneath the earth, carrying no ambition except to become what Love has always imagined.

> *Still my heart until Your way becomes clear.*
