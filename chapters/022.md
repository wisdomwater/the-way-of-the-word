## 22 – The Life That Lives Through You

A crooked branch does not apologize to the sky. It bends toward the light in the shape the seasons have given it. An empty bowl does not mourn what it lacks; its emptiness is what allows it to receive. So much of life seems to arrive by this strange reversal. We become whole when we stop hiding the unfinished places. We become full when we make room. We discover ourselves when we loosen our grip upon the person we have been trying so hard to become. The Sage walks quietly through the village without announcing his wisdom. He does not polish his goodness until everyone can see their reflection in it. He simply lives. And because he is no longer protecting an image of himself, something clear begins to shine through him, like morning sunlight passing through an open window.

Perhaps this is why the Carpenter spoke so often of losing life in order to find it. A seed disappears beneath the earth before the green shoot rises. Nets are left upon the shore before fishermen discover another kind of abundance. Bread is broken before it can feed the gathered table. Again and again, life seems to whisper that what we surrender is not always lost. There is a deeper self beneath the one we labor to construct—a life hidden in the Beloved, waiting beneath our ambitions, defenses, and carefully guarded names. I have sometimes wondered whether holiness is simply becoming transparent to that Life. We cease asking the river where it is taking us. We loosen our feet from the riverbed. And one morning we discover that we are no longer carrying ourselves toward God. We are being carried.

> *Empty me into the life You give.*
