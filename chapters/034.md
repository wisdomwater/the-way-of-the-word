## 34 – The Greatness That Kneels

The river does not stand beside the field asking whether the wheat remembers where its water came from. Rain falls upon cedar and weed alike, then disappears into the soil without leaving its name upon the roots. Everywhere I look, the deepest gifts seem strangely unconcerned with being noticed. The Way pours itself through the ten thousand things and then hides within them. It becomes blossom without announcing itself as blossom, birdsong without demanding that the forest listen, breath without asking the lungs for gratitude. Perhaps this is why true greatness can be difficult to recognize. We have learned to look upward for what is great—to towers, thrones, mountains, and stars. Yet the Sage looks downward as well, toward the dark earth beneath her feet, silently nourishing everything that rises from it. What sustains the world does not seem troubled by whether the world knows its name.

And then I remember the Teacher kneeling with a basin of water before tired feet. The hands that broke bread, touched lepers, and lifted children did not seem interested in appearing great. They simply gave themselves to whatever love required. Perhaps this is what divine greatness looks like when it becomes visible: not a throne demanding that all things bow, but the Beloved bending low enough to wash the dust from our feet. I have sometimes wondered whether creation itself is such a gesture—God forever giving life without clutching it, hidden within every heartbeat yet refusing to possess what has been made. The sun does not keep its light. Bread does not remain whole if it would become nourishment. Love seems always to be pouring itself away. And strangely, nothing is diminished. The more deeply it gives, the more clearly its inexhaustible fullness appears.

> *Make me small enough for Your love to flow through.*
