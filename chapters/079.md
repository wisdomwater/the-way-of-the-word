## 79 – The Door Hidden in Failure

When a potter finds a crack in the clay, she does not scold the wheel. She wets her hands, feels where the wall has grown thin, and begins again. The Sage receives failure in much the same way. She does not waste the moment searching for someone upon whom the burden can be placed. Blame is a road without an ending; every accusation awakens another, until the whole village is pointing across the street. Instead, she turns quietly toward what belongs to her. What can be repaired? What must be admitted? What can this broken place teach? There is freedom in tending only the ground beneath our own feet. We cannot command another person's apology or awakening. We can mend our portion of the fence. We can return what we borrowed. We can speak the truth we avoided. And sometimes the place we called failure becomes the doorway through which humility enters.

I think of the Friend asking why we notice the speck in another's eye while carrying a beam in our own. Perhaps he was inviting us away from the endless courtroom of blame and back into the workshop of the heart. There, the Beloved does not humiliate us for what has gone wrong. A disciple who denied his friend beside a charcoal fire later found another fire waiting on the shore, with breakfast and a question about love. Failure was not the end of his story. It became a place of return. Perhaps grace works this way within us as well. We admit what is ours without demanding that another do the same. We repair what our hands can reach and release what they cannot. Then even regret becomes useful soil. Something gentler grows there—the freedom of no longer needing to be blameless, only willing to turn again toward love.

> *Show me what is mine to mend, and help me release the rest.*
