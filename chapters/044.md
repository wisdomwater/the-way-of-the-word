## 44 – Nothing Is Missing

A man may carry a jeweled cup to the river and still drink the same water as the traveler who kneels with empty hands. The mountain does not ask whether either is important. The pine does not lean closer to the famous, nor does the moon withhold its light from the forgotten. Much of our weariness comes from forgetting this simplicity. We gather names, possessions, praise, and accomplishments as though enough of them might finally persuade the heart that it is safe. Yet each new treasure requires another hand to guard it. The Sage begins to loosen her grip. She eats when hungry, rests when tired, receives the morning as it comes. Contentment does not mean that nothing should ever change. It is the quieter discovery that life need not become something else before it can be received. When the heart stops keeping account, the ordinary world becomes strangely abundant.

The Shepherd once pointed toward birds moving across the sky and lilies opening in the grass. Neither seemed troubled by reputation or tomorrow's storehouse. They simply received what was given and became what they were. Perhaps this is why he spoke so tenderly of treasure. The richest things could not be locked in barns: mercy, friendship, bread shared at a table, the kingdom hidden within and among us. We spend years asking the world to tell us that we are enough, while the Beloved has already called us into being without requiring an achievement in return. There is a freedom in letting that love become sufficient. The cup may still be small. The road may still contain loss. Yet when we cease measuring our lives against another imagined life, we may look around and discover something astonishing: sunlight on the table, breath in the body, love still possible—and for this moment, nothing missing.

> *Awaken me to the fullness already here.*
