## 16 – Returning

At dawn the valley seems to awaken all at once. Birds lift into the pale sky, blossoms open to the light, and the stream hurries over its ancient stones as though it has somewhere important to be. Yet as evening gathers, everything begins the quiet journey home. The birds return to their hidden branches. The flowers gently fold their petals. Even the restless wind grows soft among the bamboo before disappearing into the silence from which it first arose. I have come to believe that all of creation knows a rhythm the heart easily forgets. We spend so much of our lives reaching outward that we scarcely notice the gentle current forever drawing us inward, back toward the hidden spring from which every living thing first drank.

The Shepherd never seemed afraid of endings. A seed disappearing beneath the soil, a day's light fading beyond the hills, a loaf broken and shared among friends—nothing was truly lost. Everything was being gathered into a deeper fullness. Perhaps this is why those who learn to rest in the Beloved begin to move through the world with such quiet kindness. They no longer need to defend every opinion or secure every tomorrow. Their hearts become spacious enough to welcome both laughter and tears, success and failure, arrival and departure. Like an old grandmother whose embrace asks nothing in return, they simply make room for life. And when the final evening comes, it is received not as an exile into darkness, but as another gentle homecoming into the Love that has carried them all along.

> *Return me, Beloved, to the quiet of my Source.*
