## 23 – When the Rain Is Only Rain

There are mornings when the bamboo bends beneath a hard wind, and evenings when rain covers the garden until every leaf trembles beneath it. Yet the wind does not remain to explain itself. The rain offers no apology for having fallen. When their moment has passed, they pass, and sunlight quietly returns between the clouds. Perhaps the heart can learn something from this. Joy may be allowed to become wholly joy, sorrow wholly sorrow, silence wholly silence. We need not clutch each experience and weave it immediately into a story about who we are. Even loss can be received this way—not welcomed as something pleasant, but allowed to move through the open chambers of the heart. The river does not refuse the fallen branch. It receives it, carries it for a while, and continues toward the sea.

I remember the Friend weeping beside a tomb. There was no hurry to turn grief into a lesson. Tears were permitted to be tears. Later there would be movement, astonishment, and life, but first there was simply sorrow shared beneath the open sky. Perhaps this is part of what it means to become transparent to the Beloved: we cease resisting every weather that passes through the soul. We speak when words are given, and when they are finished, we return to quiet. We mourn when mourning comes. We laugh when laughter rises around the table. Beneath all these changing skies there remains something deeper, a Presence that neither arrives with the storm nor departs with the sunlight. When we stop demanding that every moment be different from what it is, the heart may discover that it has been held through all of them.

> *Beloved, let me receive each moment and release it.*
