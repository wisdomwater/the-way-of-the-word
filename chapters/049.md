## 49 – A Heart as Wide as the Sky

The sky does not choose which roofs may receive its rain. It stretches above the temple and the tavern, the garden and the prison, the home of the generous and the house of the cruel. Clouds pass through its openness without leaving a wound behind. The Sage cultivates a heart like this. She listens so deeply that, for a moment, another person's sorrow can find room within her own silence. She does not reserve kindness only for those who have earned it, nor allow another's unfaithfulness to decide what kind of person she will become. This does not make her naïve. She sees clearly, yet refuses to let suspicion harden her heart. Perhaps true goodness begins when goodness no longer depends upon the goodness of the one standing before us. Like the valley receiving every stream, she remains open, spacious enough to hold the world without needing to become like everything she receives.

I think of the Friend speaking of rain falling upon the just and unjust, sunlight warming fields without asking who deserves the harvest. His table seemed to share that wideness. The respectable sat there, but so did the disgraced. Children climbed near. Doubters remained close. Even the one who would betray him received bread from his hand. There is something almost frightening about such love because it cannot be controlled by our careful accounting. The Beloved does not seem to love as merchants trade, giving only where repayment is certain. Love pours itself out because love is what it is. Perhaps this is the spacious mind we are invited to inhabit—not blindness to harm, but freedom from allowing harm to dictate the boundaries of compassion. The heart becomes like a great sky beneath which every person is still someone's child, still held within a mercy wider than we can understand.

> *Make my heart as wide as Your mercy.*
