## 50 – After a Good Day’s Work

At evening, the farmer leaves the field. He does not cling to the final light or command the sun to remain above the hills. The tools are washed, the gate is closed, and what has been planted is entrusted to the darkness. The Sage learns to live with this same open hand. Each moment arrives like weather, and he gives himself to it without asking that it stay. Laughter comes; he laughs. Sorrow comes; he lets the tears have their season. Work appears; his hands meet it. Rest arrives; he sits beneath the tree. Perhaps our fear of death is tangled with all the smaller things we refuse to release—the image of ourselves we protect, the future we believe we must possess, the moments we wish could remain forever. Yet life has always been teaching us to let go. Every breath enters, nourishes us, and must be released before another can come.

The Friend once placed his life into hands he trusted, not as one surrendering to meaninglessness, but as a child returning home. Long before that final hour, he had practiced such surrender in ordinary ways: withdrawing into silence, giving bread away, allowing crowds to leave, walking toward love without demanding that love preserve him from pain. Perhaps this is how a heart becomes ready for death—by becoming completely available to life. Nothing held back. Nothing hoarded against the evening. The Beloved does not ask us to despise this beautiful world, but to receive it so deeply that we no longer need to possess it. Then death may become less like a thief tearing life from our hands and more like sleep after faithful labor: the tools laid down, the lamp extinguished, the tired body resting in a darkness already filled with Presence.

> *Teach me to hold nothing back from life.*
