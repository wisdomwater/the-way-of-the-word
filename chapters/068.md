## 68 – The Spirit of Play

Watch children race beneath the trees and you may notice something adults often forget. They run with all their strength, laughing as they pass one another, then collapse together in the grass when the race is done. For a moment there was competition, but no enemy. The Sage carries something of this lightness into every contest. He does not need another person to become smaller so that he may feel large. The skill of an opponent sharpens his own. The wisdom of another does not diminish him. Even leadership becomes a form of listening, and work becomes a way of nourishing the larger village. Competition turns poisonous only when winning becomes a verdict upon our worth. Released from that burden, we can give ourselves fully to the game and still rejoice when another plays beautifully. Perhaps the deepest victory is to contend without allowing the heart to become divided.

The Friend's companions sometimes seemed eager to know who stood highest among them, but he kept returning them to children. Perhaps children understand something about the kingdom before we teach them to measure themselves against everyone else. They can build a tower together, knock it down, argue over a stone, and moments later be laughing again. The game has not yet become an identity. I wonder how much lighter our lives would become if we remembered this. We could work wholeheartedly without making success our name. We could disagree without turning one another into enemies. We could even meet those who oppose us with curiosity, wondering what truth becomes visible from where they stand. The Beloved has no shortage of light, so another person's brightness need not threaten our own. There is room in the field for many children to run. When the race is finished, perhaps we will discover that we were playing together all along.

> *Teach me to give my best without needing another to lose.*
