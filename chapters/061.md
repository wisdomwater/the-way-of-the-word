## 61 – The Strength to Bow

Every stream seeks the low places. Snow melts from the high mountains, rain gathers on the hillsides, and all of it descends until the great sea receives what the smallest creek carries. Perhaps this is why the sea can become so vast: it does not insist upon being above anything. The Sage understands that true greatness bends downward. The more strength she is given, the less she needs to defend herself. When someone reveals her error, she listens as though receiving water from an unexpected spring. She names what was wrong, repairs what she can, and continues without constructing excuses around her pride. Even an enemy may become a strange teacher, revealing a shadow she could not see while standing in her own light. Humility does not make her small. It makes her spacious. Because she does not spend her strength protecting an image of herself, she remains free to change.

I think of the Friend kneeling before those who called him Teacher, wrapping a towel around his waist and washing the dust from their feet. Greatness had descended to the lowest place in the room. Perhaps this is why his strength still feels so different from the power we usually seek. It did not need to silence critics, defend status, or make others smaller. The kingdom he embodied seemed to spread by nourishment rather than domination—bread multiplied, wounds touched, outsiders welcomed, burdens lifted. I wonder whether every life becomes luminous in the same way. We do not shine by standing above others, but by becoming low enough for their rivers to reach us. We listen when corrected. We bless those who reveal our shadows. We tend the small piece of earth entrusted to us. And like the sea, we grow larger by learning to bow.

> *Make me humble enough to receive every stream.*
