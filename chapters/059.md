## 59 – The Spacious Heart

The sky does not insist that every cloud take the same shape. Sunlight enters the valley wherever an opening appears. The mountain stands without stubbornness, while the willow survives the storm by knowing how to bend. The Sage gathers these quiet lessons into his way of living. He is firm where firmness is needed and supple where resistance would only break the branch. Most of all, he has learned to hold his own ideas lightly. A plan may guide the morning, but he does not demand that evening resemble it. When the road turns unexpectedly, he turns with it. When rain changes the journey, he listens to the rain. Moderation gives him room to respond rather than react. Because he is no longer dragging life toward a fixed destination, almost anything life brings can become part of the Way. His strength comes not from possessing every answer, but from remaining spacious enough to receive what arrives.

The Shepherd carried a similar spaciousness among those who gathered around him. A meal became a place of belonging. An interruption became a healing. A child wandering into the circle became a teacher. Again and again, what others might have treated as inconvenience became an opening for love. Perhaps this is what happens when we loosen our grip upon how things are supposed to unfold. The Beloved becomes visible in what we had not planned. And from this freedom grows a gentler kind of care—not management from above, but tenderness from within. Like a mother waking in the night at the sound of her child, love responds because another life has become precious to her. It does not need a grand destination. It simply attends to what is here. Perhaps the deepest leadership is nothing more mysterious than this: an uncluttered heart, open hands, and enough freedom to serve the life directly before us.

> *Make my heart spacious enough for what this moment brings.*
