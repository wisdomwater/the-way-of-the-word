## 38 – Beneath the Flower

An orchard does not strain to appear fruitful. In spring it wears blossoms, but the blossom is not the purpose of the tree. Petals must fall. What matters is happening deeper—in sap rising through hidden channels, roots drinking from dark soil, fruit slowly gathering sweetness beneath the sun. The Sage lives close to these depths. He has little interest in appearing virtuous, powerful, or wise. Goodness that must continually announce itself has already begun to lose its fragrance. When the living stream dries, we build channels and paint signs telling people where water once flowed. Rules multiply. Ritual becomes performance. Hands begin forcing what hearts no longer understand. Yet somewhere beneath all this, the spring remains. Perhaps the way home is not to decorate the empty channel, but to dig again toward the water—to leave appearances behind and descend into the quiet place from which a true life rises naturally.

The Friend seemed forever brushing aside the flower to reveal the fruit. A polished cup mattered less than what it held. Long prayers mattered less than the heart that prayed them. Sacred days were meant to give life, not become burdens laid upon weary shoulders. He did not discard the outward things; he simply would not confuse the vessel with the wine. Perhaps every spiritual path faces this temptation. We begin with wonder, then try to preserve the wonder in forms. The forms become precious, and one day we may find ourselves defending the lamp while forgetting the flame. Yet the Beloved keeps drawing us inward, beneath performance and reputation, beneath even our effort to become good. There, in the hidden room of the heart, love needs no audience. Mercy does not know it is merciful. The branch simply remains in the vine, and in its season, fruit appears.

> *Lead me beneath appearances to the living root.*
