## 48 – The Wisdom of Less

The scholar’s room slowly fills with books, notes, maps, and carefully labeled jars. Each day something new is placed upon the shelf. But the Sage’s room seems to grow emptier. One day she releases the need to be admired. Another day, the need to be right. Later she notices an old resentment she has carried for years and sets that down as well. The journey becomes strangely light. This does not mean she stops learning or caring. She simply discovers that wisdom is sometimes revealed by subtraction. A stream runs clear when we stop stirring its bottom. A garden knows how to grow without our pulling at its stems. Slowly the hands unclench. We interfere less, listen more, and begin to sense the hidden rhythm already moving beneath things. Eventually action becomes so natural that it no longer feels like forcing. The work is done, yet the worker has almost disappeared.

I think of the Friend sending his companions onto the road with very little: no overflowing pack, no carefully guarded provisions, no storehouse against every imaginable need. Travel lightly. Receive what comes. Trust what is given. There is something of the inner life here as well. We begin prayer carrying many things—our ideas about God, our expectations of ourselves, our methods, fears, answers, and ambitions. Over time, the Beloved seems gently to empty our hands. Words become fewer. Certainties soften. Even the desire to manufacture a spiritual experience begins to fade. What remains may look like almost nothing: a breath, a surrendered heart, a willingness to be present. Yet perhaps this emptiness is not poverty. It is availability. When we stop trying to make grace happen, we may discover that grace has been quietly happening all along, accomplishing what our striving could never complete.

> *Teach me the freedom of letting go.*
