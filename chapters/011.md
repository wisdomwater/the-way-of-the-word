The old wheelwright smiled as he held up a finished wheel to the morning sun. His hands had labored over every spoke, every curve of the rim, every careful measure of wood, yet his finger rested in the quiet circle at its heart. "Here," he whispered, though no one stood beside him. The mountain stream seemed to nod in agreement as it slipped around stones without keeping any for itself. A tea bowl waits in the same way. Its beauty is not merely the clay that catches the eye, but the stillness it protects within. The bamboo forest knows this secret as well. It rises because each hollow stem welcomes the wind instead of resisting it. Perhaps the deepest gifts are not fashioned by what we gather, but by the room we quietly leave for grace to enter.

As the years pass, the heart begins to resemble such a dwelling. We spend our youth furnishing every chamber with opinions, ambitions, and careful certainties, until one day the Beloved gently opens a window and lets the unnecessary drift away like autumn leaves crossing the lake. The Shepherd does not seem eager to fill every silence with instruction. More often He sits beside the fire, breaks bread among friends, and allows the warmth between them to speak what words cannot. It may be that prayer is less the adding of holy thoughts than the widening of an inner room where Love has always waited to abide. The empty hands receive. The quiet heart becomes a home. And in that hidden space, life begins to move with a gentleness that no striving could ever create.

> *Make room within.*
