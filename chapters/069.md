## 69 – The Courage to Yield

Two rams meeting upon a narrow mountain path may lower their heads and discover that neither can pass. The Sage knows another possibility. Sometimes one step backward creates more room than ten steps forward. He does not confuse restraint with cowardice or yielding with surrender to what is harmful. He simply refuses to let opposition choose the shape of his heart. The moment we decide that the person before us is nothing but evil, something precious begins to disappear. Simplicity becomes suspicion. Patience becomes urgency. Compassion closes its door. Soon we resemble the very thing we oppose. So the Sage waits before striking, listens before answering, and leaves space where another path might appear. The willow survives the storm not because it defeats the wind, but because it knows how to bend without surrendering its roots. There is courage in remaining soft when hardness would be easier.

In the garden, when frightened hands reached for a sword, the Friend asked them to put it away. He did not pretend that darkness was harmless; he simply refused to let darkness teach him how to become dark. Even the wounded enemy received his touch. Perhaps this is among the most difficult movements of love—to resist what destroys without learning to hate the one through whom destruction comes. The Beloved seems to guard something deeper than victory: the soul of both the wounded and the one who wounds. There are times when love must stand firm, speak clearly, or place itself between harm and the vulnerable. Yet even then, it need not surrender its three treasures. We can oppose without contempt, retreat without shame, and yield without abandoning truth. Sometimes the deepest victory is simply this: the conflict ends, and compassion is still alive within us.

> *Let no enemy take compassion from my heart.*
