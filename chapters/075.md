## 75 – The Mercy of Making Room

A garden can be tended until it can no longer breathe. Too much pruning wounds the branches; too much water drowns the roots. Even a hand intending kindness can become heavy when it never withdraws. The Sage understands that care must leave room for life to unfold. She tends what truly needs tending, then steps back. She trusts the farmer to know his field, the artisan to know her hands, the family to find its own rhythm around the evening table. There is a generosity in not managing everything. The great sky shelters the valley without pressing down upon it. The river nourishes its banks without telling each reed how to grow. Perhaps good leadership resembles this spaciousness. It protects without smothering, serves without possessing, and gives enough room for people to rediscover the strength already living within them. Sometimes the kindest hand is the one that knows when to open.

The Shepherd cared deeply for those entrusted to him, yet his care did not resemble a cage. He fed, healed, taught, and then sent people back into their lives. Those who followed him were invited rather than dragged. Even the rich young man who turned away was allowed to walk his own road. There is something tender in such freedom. The Beloved seems unwilling to secure our devotion by making choice impossible. Love makes room, and in that room we slowly become capable of answering love ourselves. Perhaps this is why grace can feel so quiet. It does not hover anxiously over every movement of the soul. It accompanies, whispers, waits, and trusts what has been planted. We may do something similar for one another: offer bread where there is hunger, shelter where there is danger, wisdom where it is welcomed—and then leave enough open sky for each person to stand, breathe, and grow.

> *Teach me to care without taking away another’s freedom.*
