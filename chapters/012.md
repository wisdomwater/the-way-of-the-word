Morning arrives quietly over the hills. Mist lingers among the bamboo while the first birds offer only a few simple notes before the world grows busy with its own noise. The pond reflects the whole sky without trying to possess a single cloud. Yet as the day unfolds, bright banners begin to flutter, merchants raise their voices, fragrant meals call from every doorway, and the mind rushes after each invitation like leaves caught in a restless wind. It may be that the eye grows weary not because beauty has become scarce, but because it has forgotten how to rest. The deepest seeing belongs to those who can sit beside a small stream until its gentle song becomes more satisfying than every clamor that once demanded attention.

The Friend often seemed unhurried among hurried people. He noticed sparrows no one else saw, lilies that opened without applause, fishermen mending torn nets in the fading light. His gaze did not cling, nor did it turn away. It rested upon each moment as though everything already belonged to the Father's quiet keeping. Perhaps this is the hidden freedom of the open heart. Joy comes, and it is welcomed like a traveler at the gate. Sorrow comes, and a place is set for it beside the fire. Neither is asked to remain. Little by little the soul becomes like the evening sky—vast enough for every passing cloud, yet never mistaken for the clouds themselves. Within such spaciousness, the Beloved has always been near.

> *Open my heart as wide as the sky.*
