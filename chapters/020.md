## 20 – Drinking from the Great Mother

There were years when I wished to walk as confidently as everyone else. The merchants seemed certain of their bargains. The scholars carried answers as though they were lanterns that could never go out. Even the pilgrims compared the distance they had traveled, measuring one another by how quickly they climbed the mountain. Yet I often found myself lingering beside streams, watching fallen leaves circle in quiet eddies for no reason I could explain. There were days I felt as rootless as a feather carried by the wind, belonging nowhere except wherever the breeze happened to rest. It troubled me once. Now I wonder if the valley appears empty only because it has learned to receive every river without trying to become one itself.

The Beloved, too, was not always understood. Some mistook His gentleness for weakness and His silence for uncertainty. Yet He seemed content to remain hidden like a seed beneath the earth, trusting a life no eye could yet perceive. There is a freedom that arrives when we no longer hunger to be admired, nor fear being overlooked. The heart becomes wonderfully light, like an infant asleep against its mother's breast, needing nothing beyond the quiet rhythm of love itself. Perhaps this is the beginning of true wisdom—not possessing certainty, but resting deeply enough to be carried. Then we cease chasing the applause of passing crowds and discover that the only nourishment we have ever truly needed has been flowing toward us since before our first breath, and will continue long after our last.

> *Let me rest in the quiet that nourishes my soul.*
