## 65 – The Grace of Not-Knowing

There is a clarity that comes when the cup is emptied. The beginner enters the garden without naming every flower, and because she does not yet know what should be there, she notices what the expert may pass by. The Sage protects something of this openness. He does not hurry to fill every silence with an answer or turn mystery into a collection of conclusions. Cleverness can become another possession, something carried before us like a lantern so bright that we no longer see the stars. But not-knowing softens the eyes. It allows the path to reveal itself beneath our feet. The ordinary life becomes sufficient again: rice steaming in a bowl, sandals beside the door, a neighbor calling across the fence. Perhaps wisdom is not the accumulation of certainty, but the gradual release of our need to possess it. When we no longer insist that we know the way, we become teachable by the Way itself.

I think of the Friend placing a child among those who wished to know who was greatest. A child does not come carrying a system by which heaven can be mastered. She receives. She wonders. She asks without embarrassment and rests without needing every question resolved. There is something of the kingdom in that unguarded mind. The old contemplatives eventually discovered the same country and called it unknowing: a darkness entered not because God is absent, but because the Beloved exceeds the reach of our thoughts. Perhaps our finest ideas are fingers pointing toward a moon they can never hold. So we let them point, and then we become quiet. Beneath all we have learned, something simpler waits—the true self before performance, the heart before certainty, the life already held within God. We do not need to become clever enough to find our way home. We may only need to become simple enough to recognize it.

> *Empty me of certainty and keep me open to wonder.*
