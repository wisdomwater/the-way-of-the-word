## 2 – The Quiet Hands

Morning light does not choose where it will fall. It rests with equal tenderness upon the wildflower opening beside the path and the weathered stone half hidden beneath moss. The stream does not argue with the fallen branch that slows its course, nor does the mountain envy the valley that gathers every wandering river. I have watched bamboo bow beneath winter snow until one could scarcely believe it would rise again. Yet when spring returned, it did not remember the weight that had bent it. Perhaps the heart begins to soften in much the same way. It no longer spends its strength dividing the world into what deserves love and what does not. It simply receives each moment as the dawn receives another day.

I once sat with an old brother in a hillside monastery while bread cooled upon the windowsill and sparrows quarreled in the fig tree beyond. We spoke very little. He smiled as though silence itself were enough conversation, then broke the loaf and placed the larger piece into my hands without noticing he had done so. Years later, I can no longer remember his words, only the fragrance of warm bread and the kindness that asked for nothing in return. It may be that this is the hidden work of the Beloved. Seeds are scattered without counting where they fall. Feet are washed without seeking gratitude. Love moves quietly through the world, and because it forgets itself, it remains when louder things have faded.

> *May my hands become quiet.*
