## 24 – Do the Work, Then Release It

I once watched a heron standing at the edge of a mountain stream. It did not stretch itself taller to impress the reeds, nor hurry from stone to stone searching for a better place to stand. It simply stood where its feet had been given ground. There is a quiet strength in such simplicity. We exhaust ourselves when we rise onto tiptoe, reaching for recognition, trying to arrive before our own footsteps. We name ourselves wise, successful, useful, important—and soon must spend our strength defending the name. The Sage seems to travel lighter. He does what the hour asks of him, then leaves the work behind like footprints beside the river. Some will remain for a while. Others will disappear with the evening rain. Neither belongs to him. The path continues, and he continues with it.

The Carpenter knew this freedom. He scattered stories like seeds and allowed them to fall where they would. He healed and sometimes slipped quietly away. He washed feet without needing the room to understand what the gesture meant. Even the bread placed into his hands was blessed, broken, given, and released. Perhaps our work becomes most beautiful when we stop asking it to tell us who we are. The Gardener tends the soil faithfully, but cannot command the seed to open. The fisherman casts his net, but cannot order the lake to fill it. We are given our small portion of the work: to love, to make, to mend, to plant. Then evening comes, and the tools are laid down. What grows afterward belongs to a Mystery larger than our striving. Perhaps peace begins when we discover that this is enough.

> *Beloved, teach me to work faithfully and let go.*
