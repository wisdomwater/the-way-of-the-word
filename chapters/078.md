## 78 – The Strength of Water

Place your hand into a stream and the water yields without resistance. Close your fingers around it and it slips away. Yet return after a thousand years and the same soft water may have carved a passage through stone. The Sage learns from this patient strength. He does not meet every hardness by becoming harder. When sorrow enters, he allows himself to feel it without letting it become bitterness. When cruelty appears, he refuses to give it another home within his own heart. This is not passivity. Water moves. It carries, cleanses, nourishes, wears away what cannot forever remain. But it does so without hatred for the stone. Perhaps gentleness becomes powerful precisely because it does not need immediate victory. It can remain present longer than anger can burn. It can wait beside what is wounded until healing begins in its own hidden time.

The Friend knelt before a basin and poured water over dusty feet, even feet belonging to those who would soon fail him. Later, when violence gathered around him, he did not allow violence to dictate what his heart would become. There is a mystery here I still do not know how to explain. We often imagine that helping means taking hold of another person’s life and pulling it toward what we believe is good. Yet the Beloved sometimes helps by remaining near—without forcing, fixing, or fleeing. A grieving sister receives tears before answers. A wounded stranger receives touch before explanation. Perhaps the deepest help is sometimes a presence soft enough to make room for another person’s own life to emerge. Like water, love does not always remove the stone. It stays. It flows. And slowly, what seemed immovable begins to change.

> *Make me gentle enough to endure without becoming hard.*
