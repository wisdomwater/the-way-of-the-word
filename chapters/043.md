## 43 – The Strength of Gentleness

Water enters places stone cannot. It slips through the smallest opening, gathers in hidden cracks, and waits. It has no fist with which to strike the mountain, yet given enough time, valleys appear. Wind has no hands, yet the bamboo bends beneath it. Light has no weight, yet it enters a darkened room through the narrowest seam. The Sage trusts these quiet powers. She does not assume that everything must be changed by pressure. Sometimes the deepest movement comes through what seems to do nothing at all: presence without interference, patience without demand, silence without withdrawal. We are accustomed to measuring power by what it can move, command, or overcome. Yet perhaps there is another kind of strength—the strength of making space. The empty valley receives the river. The open hand receives the world. What does not force may enter where force could never go.

I think of the Friend writing quietly in the dust while angry voices gathered around a frightened woman. He did not answer their violence with greater violence. Something in his stillness changed the air. Stones remained in hands for a while, and then one by one they fell to the ground. There are teachings spoken like this, without argument. A basin of water beside tired feet. A hand resting upon an untouchable shoulder. Bread given to someone hungry. Silence offered to someone grieving. Perhaps the most enduring sermons are sometimes lives through which love has become visible. The Beloved does not always batter down the locked doors within us. Often grace comes like morning light beneath them, waiting without accusation until we notice that the room is no longer entirely dark. And slowly, almost without knowing how, the heart opens from within.

> *Make my gentleness spacious enough for love to enter.*
