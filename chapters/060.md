## 60 – The Wisdom of Not Poking

Anyone who has cooked a small fish knows how easily care becomes interference. Turn it too often and the flesh begins to break. Poke at it to see whether it is ready, then poke again, and soon what needed only warmth and patience has been spoiled by anxious hands. The Sage recognizes this temptation far beyond the kitchen. We meddle with people, plans, even our own souls because stillness feels irresponsible. We keep touching the wound to see whether it has healed. We argue with what we oppose until our resistance gives it a strange kind of strength. Yet there are times when wisdom simply removes its hands. The muddy pond clears when no one stirs it. A flame deprived of fuel eventually disappears. Not every darkness must be wrestled to the ground. Some things lose their power when we stop giving them something to fight.

The Friend sometimes answered hostility in this unexpected way. He could speak sharply when love required it, yet he did not seem compelled to enter every argument placed before him. Some accusations met silence. Some traps received a question. When hatred demanded an enemy, he refused to become one. Perhaps this is one of the hidden powers of peace: it denies violence the mirror it needs to multiply itself. The Beloved does not ask us to pretend evil is harmless or to remain passive before suffering. Love may stand firmly between the wolf and the lamb. But even then, it need not become the wolf. There is a still center from which resistance can arise without hatred, where we refuse harm without allowing harm to remake us in its image. Sometimes the strongest answer is an open hand that gives darkness nothing upon which to fasten.

> *Teach me when to act and when to leave darkness unfed.*
