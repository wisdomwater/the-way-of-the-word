## 67 – Three Treasures

After many years of walking, the traveler discovers that he needs less than he once imagined. The pack grows lighter. The map becomes simpler. At last, perhaps, only three treasures remain: simplicity, patience, compassion. Simplicity returns us to the clear water beneath our tangled thoughts, where this breath and this step are enough. Patience teaches us to walk beside the slow river rather than dragging it toward the sea. Compassion softens the boundary between our own suffering and the suffering of another. The Sage keeps these treasures close, especially when life becomes difficult. He is patient not only with the friend who understands him, but with the one who does not. He offers compassion not only outward, but toward the frightened and unfinished places within himself. What sounds impractical from a distance becomes sturdy beneath the feet. These simple things grow roots deep enough to survive storms.

The Friend carried few things along the roads of Galilee, yet these same treasures seemed to travel with him. His life was simple enough to notice lilies and sparrows. His patience made room for slow disciples, persistent questions, and those who misunderstood him again and again. His compassion crossed boundaries others had carefully guarded, yet it also allowed him to withdraw into quiet when his own body needed rest. Perhaps the way of love is not nearly as complicated as we make it. We need not understand every mystery before we begin. We can simplify the next action, become patient with the person before us, and extend mercy even toward the self we are still learning to accept. From these small movements, reconciliation quietly widens. The heart becomes less divided. The stranger becomes neighbor. And somewhere beneath everything, we find ourselves returning to the Source from which compassion first flowed.

> *Keep me simple, patient, and compassionate.*
