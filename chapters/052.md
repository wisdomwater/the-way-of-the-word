## 52 – Returning to the Source of Light

Follow a stream backward and the journey becomes quieter. The broad river narrows between stones, then becomes a ribbon beneath the trees, then a trickle descending from somewhere hidden in the mountain. The Sage follows life in this way. She looks at blossoms and wonders about roots, feels sunlight upon her face and remembers the unseen fire from which it came. Every manifestation becomes a footprint leading toward the mystery that gives it life. Yet we easily lose the trail when we begin dividing everything too quickly—beautiful and ugly, worthy and unworthy, desirable and unwanted. The mind fills with judgments, the senses chase one bright thing after another, and soon the quiet spring is forgotten. Perhaps clarity comes when we become willing to enter the darkness beneath appearances. There, where certainty fades, another kind of seeing begins. We discover that yielding can carry us where grasping never could.

There is an ancient tenderness in the words, *In the beginning*. I hear them and imagine a dawn before every dawn, when the Word rested with God and all things waited within that hidden communion. Then light, cedar, sparrow, ocean, human face—each flowing outward like children from an inexhaustible heart. Perhaps the contemplative journey is simply learning to follow these beloved forms home. The beauty of another person, bread upon the table, the warmth of sunlight, even the longing within our own chest can become windows through which the Source shines. We do not need to despise the world to find God. We look through it, gently, until the light within things leads us toward the Light from which they came. And perhaps eternity is not merely endless time. Perhaps it is this continual returning—light recognizing Light, the child remembering the Mother, the heart discovering the home it never truly left.

> *Lead the light within me home to its Source.*
