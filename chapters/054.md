## 54 – Roots Beneath the House

A tree survives the storm by what cannot be seen. Its branches may twist beneath the wind, leaves may scatter across the ground, even a limb may break, yet deep beneath the soil the roots remain wrapped around darkness and stone. The Sage tends this hidden place. She knows that harmony cannot be imposed upon the world from above; it must grow outward from somewhere within. First the heart becomes rooted. Then something of that rootedness enters the home, the village, the wider land. Like circles spreading from a pebble dropped into still water, what is cultivated in secret does not remain there. A peaceful person changes the room she enters. A generous household becomes shelter for others. Perhaps we spend too much time trying to repair distant branches while neglecting the root beneath our own feet. The great transformation may begin very near—in the soil of the heart.

The Carpenter once spoke of branches remaining in a vine, drawing their life from something deeper than themselves. Fruit was not produced by anxious effort. It appeared because the branch remained connected to its source. I have come to think that much of the spiritual life is simply this kind of abiding. We allow the Beloved to become present in us, then trust that Presence to travel outward in ways we may never see. A patient word changes a child. A forgiven wound alters a family story. A small act of mercy may pass from life to life long after our name has been forgotten. Perhaps this is how the kingdom grows—not primarily through grand designs, but through rooted people whose hidden lives slowly become shade and nourishment for others. And when I wonder whether such quiet goodness can truly matter, I return within and listen for the Life already rising through the roots.

> *Root me deeply, and let love grow outward.*
