## 53 – The Forgotten Road

The great road is often the one beneath our feet, yet the eye is easily drawn toward glittering paths disappearing into the hills. We wander after more—more wealth, more power, more comfort—until abundance gathers in a few houses while other tables remain empty. The Sage notices when the river of life has been dammed. Water that should nourish the whole valley has been gathered behind private walls, and downstream the soil begins to crack. This is not merely an imbalance of possessions; something deeper has been forgotten. We belong to one another. The rice in my bowl depends upon hands I may never see. The roof above me rests upon gifts I did not create. When prosperity forgets relationship, it becomes strangely poor. The Way returns us to simplicity, where enough is recognized as enough and what overflows naturally finds its way toward those who thirst.

The Shepherd seemed especially attentive to those left beside the road. Hungry people were not abstractions to him. The poor were not problems to be solved from a comfortable distance. He sat among them, touched them, ate with them, and spoke of a kingdom where the last somehow found themselves near the front. I think also of bread placed into his hands, blessed and broken until everyone on the hillside had eaten. No one was asked whether hunger had been earned. There was bread, there were empty hands, and love moved between them. Perhaps justice begins in such simple seeing. The Beloved draws our gaze away from glittering storehouses and back toward the human face before us. When one table groans beneath excess while another child goes hungry, the great harmony has been forgotten. The way home may begin simply: open the storehouse, lengthen the table, remember that the bread was always gift.

> *Teach me the simple road where no one is forgotten.*
