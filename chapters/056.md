## 56 – Beneath the Dust

There comes a time when even beautiful words must be allowed to fall silent. The tea has been poured; the fire burns low; outside, wind moves softly through the bamboo. Nothing more needs to be explained. The Sage closes his mouth and lets the world return to itself. The sharp edges of opinion begin to soften. Knots loosen because he no longer pulls against them. The dust stirred by endless wanting and judging slowly settles, and beneath it something ancient appears—not a new identity to acquire, but the life that was there before all the names. Praise cannot add to it. Disgrace cannot take it away. It does not move closer when admired or farther away when ignored. Like the valley receiving rain, it simply gives itself. Perhaps our deepest self is discovered in the same way: not by constructing something greater, but by becoming quiet enough for what is already true to emerge.

The Friend also knew when words had reached their edge. Before accusation he sometimes remained silent. Before grief he wept. Before mystery he withdrew into the hills and prayed beneath the stars. There are places where explanations become too small, where the Beloved can only be received. The old contemplatives called this a cloud of unknowing, but perhaps even that is one name too many. We enter it by releasing our need to grasp God, defend ourselves, or turn every experience into meaning. Then prayer becomes less like speaking across a distance and more like resting within a Presence that cannot be approached because it was never absent. Honor comes and goes. Failure comes and goes. Even the identities we have carried for decades loosen their hold. Beneath them remains the quiet gift of being—received moment by moment from the One who continually gives Himself away.

> *Settle my dust until only Your quiet remains.*
