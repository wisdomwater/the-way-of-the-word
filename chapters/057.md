## 57 – As Common as Grass

A gardener can become so anxious for the garden that he never allows it to grow. He trims every wandering vine, straightens every stem, measures every patch of earth, and soon the garden bears more of his fear than its own life. The Sage leads with lighter hands. She makes room for what is good to arise without constantly commanding its shape. Too many fences teach the eye to search for gates. Too much protection can make the village afraid. Even our desire to improve others may become another way of refusing to trust the life within them. So she steps back. Not from care, but from control. She tends the soil, removes what truly chokes life, then allows sun and rain to do what no decree can accomplish. And one morning she notices that goodness has appeared everywhere, quietly and without permission, as ordinary as grass between the stones.

The Friend seemed to trust this hidden goodness more than systems built to contain it. He placed compassion above regulations when the two collided, healed on forbidden days, and spoke of sacred law ripening into love within the heart. Even religion could become another fence when people forgot the field it was meant to protect. Perhaps this is why he kept returning to seeds, vineyards, yeast, and children—things that grow from within rather than by command. The Beloved does not seem to force holiness upon us. Grace works more quietly, inviting freedom until goodness becomes less like obedience to an external rule and more like fruit appearing naturally upon a living branch. We still need wisdom, boundaries, and care. Yet beneath them all must remain spaciousness. For when love is truly rooted in the heart, no one needs to order the grass to grow.

> *Loosen my need to control and let love grow freely.*
