## 3 – The Empty Bowl

There is a tea bowl I have carried for many years. Its glaze is cracked by countless seasons of hot water and cool mornings, and one small chip rests along its rim where it slipped from my hands onto a stone floor. Visitors sometimes overlook it in favor of polished cups that gleam in the sunlight. Yet whenever I pour the tea into that old bowl, it receives the warmth without complaint. It asks for nothing more than to be empty. The valley gathers rivers for the same reason. The open hand receives what the clenched fist cannot. Perhaps the heart grows spacious not by collecting treasures, but by quietly laying them down until there is room again for the wind to pass through.

I remember walking the hills above the lake with an old shepherd whose cloak had been mended so many times that no one could tell where the original cloth remained. He knew every bend in the path, yet he never hurried ahead of the slowest lamb. At midday we shared coarse bread, olives, and cool water from a spring hidden beneath fig trees. Nothing about the meal appeared remarkable, but I left with the strange feeling that I had feasted more deeply than at tables heavy with silver. It may be that the Beloved fills us only as much as we are willing to become empty. When certainty loosens its grip and ambition grows quiet, the soul discovers a hidden strength, gentle enough to carry others without ever needing to be seen.

> *Empty me for Your fullness.*
