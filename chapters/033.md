## 33 – The Treasure Within

It is easier to watch another person crossing the river than to feel the current beneath our own feet. We learn the habits of those around us, recognize their fears, predict their desires, and perhaps begin to imagine that such understanding makes us wise. Yet the Sage eventually turns the lantern inward. There he finds a country more mysterious than any he has traveled. Desire rises like mist. Anger flashes and disappears. Old wounds speak in voices that once seemed to belong to others. To know these movements without becoming their servant is a quieter kind of power. And beneath them all, something simple waits. Enough. The tea in the cup. The rice in the bowl. The evening wind through an open window. The breath presently filling the lungs. Richness may begin when the heart stops measuring what is here against what is somewhere else.

There is another surrender waiting beneath this one. Every leaf eventually loosens from its branch. Every river reaches the sea. Even the hands folded in prayer must one day release the life they have been holding. The Friend did not hide this from those who walked beside him. He spoke of seeds falling into earth, of losing life and finding it, and then walked himself into the darkness from which his companions fled. Yet beyond that darkness came a morning garden, dew upon the grass, and a familiar voice speaking a woman's name. Perhaps embracing death does not mean loving our disappearance. It may mean trusting that nothing surrendered into the Beloved is finally lost. When we no longer spend every moment defending ourselves from life's ending, we may finally become available to life itself—this breath, this meal, this face before us, this astonishing and unrepeatable now.

> *Teach me to receive this life and release it.*
