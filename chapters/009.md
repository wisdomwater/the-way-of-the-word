## 9 – The Unclenched Hand

One evening I watched an elderly potter carry a newly fired bowl from his kiln. He did not fill it to its rim to admire his craftsmanship, nor did he polish away every small imperfection. He simply smiled, poured a little tea into it, and set it between us. The bowl seemed most beautiful because it still had room to receive. Nearby, the orchard bent beneath the weight of ripening fruit. Branches that held too tightly began to crack, while those that released their harvest swayed lightly again in the evening breeze. The earth has a gentle way of reminding us that enough is often quieter than more. Perhaps peace begins the moment the hand no longer closes around what it fears to lose.

I have remembered many acts of love, but rarely the faces that noticed them. The Friend never seemed hurried to preserve a reputation. Bread was broken, feet were washed, tears were gathered, and then the path continued toward another village beneath another sunrise. The flowers beside the road did not bloom for passing applause, nor did the stars wait for someone to thank them before appearing at dusk. It may be that the soul becomes free in the same hidden manner. We offer what has been placed within our hands, then quietly leave the rest to the One who tends every seed after the sower has gone home. There is a deep rest in knowing that love does not need to be remembered in order to endure.

> *Teach me to let go.*
