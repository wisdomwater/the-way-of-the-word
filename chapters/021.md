## 21 – The Light That Cannot Be Held

There is a kind of knowing that loosens its hands. I have watched a stream curl around a stone without needing to understand the stone, and bamboo lean beneath the rain without asking where the wind began. The Sage seems radiant in this same way—not because she has grasped the hidden Way, but because she has ceased trying to hold it. The deepest things cannot be carried like possessions. Cup your hands around water too tightly and it escapes between your fingers. Open them, and a small pool gathers in your palms, reflecting the moon. Perhaps wisdom begins there. Not in having the right idea about the mystery, but in becoming spacious enough for mystery to pass through us. What is dark need not be feared. Sometimes darkness is simply depth beyond the reach of our eyes.

I have felt something similar beneath olive trees at evening, when the hills grow quiet and even prayer seems too loud. The Beloved never seemed eager to give people definitions of God. He pointed instead toward seeds disappearing into earth, birds crossing the open sky, children receiving what they had not earned, bread passed from hand to hand. The kingdom was near enough to touch and yet impossible to contain. Perhaps this is why the pure in heart are said to see God. Purity may be less about possessing certainty than releasing whatever clouds our seeing. Beneath our names for God, beneath even our finest thoughts about God, there remains the One who was before our first word and will remain after our last. And sometimes, when the mind grows quiet, the heart recognizes what it cannot explain.

> *Loosen my hands and let Your light arise.*
