## 19 – The Center of the Circle

An old potter once invited me into his workshop without saying a word. Shelves were lined with bowls of every shape, yet he seemed far more interested in the empty space where he worked than in the vessels themselves. His hands moved without hurry, and the wheel turned with quiet faithfulness beneath them. Nothing was forced. Nothing strained to become beautiful. Even the silence seemed to have its own gentle craft. I realized before long that the peace I felt had little to do with the clay and much to do with the still center around which everything revolved. The wheel could spin because its heart remained still. Perhaps the soul, too, forgets its way whenever it leaves that hidden center in search of itself.

The Beloved often slipped away from the crowds when they wanted to make more of Him than Love desired. He sought lonely hillsides, quiet gardens, and the hush before dawn, returning with a heart untouched by the noise that so easily gathers around holy things. There is a curious freedom in no longer needing to appear wise, righteous, or important. Like a branch abiding in the vine, life simply flows where it is welcomed. The fruit does not anxiously produce itself, nor does the lamp struggle to shine. It may be that the truest transformation begins when we cease arranging ourselves before God and simply remain with Him. Resting there, the right word arrives without striving, the gentle act unfolds without calculation, and the heart discovers that it has been carried all along by a Love that never needed to announce itself.

> *Keep me in Your quiet center.*
