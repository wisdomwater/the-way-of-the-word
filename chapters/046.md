## 46 – Beyond the Walls of Fear

A field can receive iron in many forms. One piece becomes a plow and opens the earth for seed. Another becomes a blade raised against a stranger. The metal does not choose; the heart does. Fear has a way of reshaping everything it touches. A neighbor becomes a threat, a boundary becomes a wall, abundance becomes something to guard. Soon we are building fortresses around what we love, only to discover that we have locked ourselves inside them. The Sage notices fear without allowing it to become her master. She does not pretend danger is unreal, nor does she wander carelessly into harm. She simply refuses to let fear name the world for her. Beneath the armor, she remembers the human being. Beyond the wall, she remembers the field. And slowly the hands once clenched for protection become available again for planting.

The Friend walked through a world filled with soldiers, occupied villages, frightened disciples, and rumors of revolt. Yet he seemed strangely unwilling to let fear determine the shape of his life. He crossed boundaries others avoided, touched those others feared, and sat at tables where respectable people would not sit. When his companions reached for weapons, he called their hands away from them. Perhaps the peace he carried was deeper than safety. It arose from belonging so completely to the Beloved that even danger could not persuade him to stop loving. I wonder whether this is the freedom offered to us—not a promise that nothing painful will happen, but release from the fear that teaches us to see enemies everywhere. When fear loosens its grip, the world becomes spacious again. The stranger regains a face. The sword can become a plow. And the heart remembers what it was made to cultivate.

> *Free my heart from fear and return my hands to love.*
