## Epilogue

And now the old book closes.

The bamboo forest grows quiet. The teacher has said what he can say, and perhaps even that was too much. Words have carried us to the edge of the river, but they cannot enter the water for us.

That part belongs to our feet.

So go gently.

There is no need to become a sage. There is no examination waiting at the end of the road. There is only this ordinary life—the kitchen in the morning, the traffic on the way to work, the difficult conversation, the person who needs kindness, the unexpected rain, the evening meal, the quiet room before sleep.

This is where the Way waits.

Be like water.

When you meet hardness, do not assume that you must become harder. Water yields to the stone and continues its journey. It does not mistake gentleness for weakness. Given enough time, the soft thing reshapes the hard.

When the path turns, turn with it.

When the door closes, do not exhaust yourself beating against it. Perhaps there is another opening nearby. Perhaps there is a season for waiting. Perhaps the river knows a way around the mountain that you cannot yet see.

Yielding is not giving up. It is releasing the demand that life obey the map you drew before the journey began. And when something comes into your hands, let your hands remain open.

Bread becomes nourishment when it is broken and shared. Water remains fresh because it keeps flowing. The tree does not eat its own fruit. The sun does not keep its warmth for itself.

The Beloved seems to have woven generosity into the fabric of things.

So give what you can.

Give attention.

Give forgiveness.

Give patience.

Give bread.

Give the benefit of the doubt.

Give another person room to become who they are.

And when you have nothing left to give, receive.

That too is part of the Way.

Receive rest without shame. Receive kindness without immediately trying to repay it. Receive the meal, the laughter, the sunlight through the window. Receive this strange and beautiful life without demanding that it become something greater before you are willing to love it.

Perhaps this is where contentment begins.

Not in finally arranging the world according to our desires, but in loosening our desires enough to notice the world already here.

The cup of tea is warm.

The wind is moving through the trees.

Someone you love is sitting nearby.

You are breathing.

For this moment, perhaps nothing is missing.

The Carpenter once pointed toward lilies and birds, children and seeds, bread and vineyards. The old Sage pointed toward valleys, rivers, uncarved wood, and newborn children. Neither seemed particularly interested in making life more complicated.

Look closely. Receive freely. Love generously. Do not grasp. Trust what grows in secret.

And when you fail—and you will—flow around the stone and begin again.

There is room for you in this great unfolding. You do not need to stand above creation or master it. You belong within it. Your breath rises and falls beneath the same sky as every creature. The dust beneath your feet is ancient. The water within your body has traveled rivers and clouds beyond remembering. You are not a stranger dropped into the universe.

You are part of the song.

And perhaps our place in the Dao, our place within God's creation, is not something we finally discover through striving.

Perhaps it is something we remember when striving grows quiet.

We were never asked to carry the river. Only to enter it. So loosen your hands. Soften your heart. Give what has been given to you. Let yourself be carried when you are tired.

And wherever the river leads, trust that the sea is waiting.
