# The Way of the Word — A Contemplative Journey through the Dao De Jing

A contemplative devotional through all 81 chapters of the *Dao De Jing*, written from the imagined perspective of an elder who has spent a lifetime walking both the Daoist path and the Christian mystical path.

He has wandered bamboo forests and the shores of Galilee. He has listened to the wisdom of Laozi and followed the way of Jesus. Rather than attempting to collapse these traditions into one, he allows them to sit beside one another and illuminate the mystery from their own perspectives.

## About the Project

This project began with a simple question:

**What might a follower of Jesus discover by listening deeply to the *Dao De Jing*?**

The *Dao De Jing* offers a vision of life shaped by simplicity, humility, yielding, non-grasping, compassion, and *wu wei*—often translated as non-action or effortless action. Again and again, its images point toward water seeking the lowest place, valleys receiving what flows into them, trees bending in the wind, and the wise person acting without needing to possess the results.

These images sometimes find surprising echoes in the teachings and life of Jesus: lilies that do not strive, seeds growing secretly in the earth, greatness discovered in service, children embodying the kingdom, enemies met with love, and a Teacher kneeling to wash the feet of his friends.

This devotional explores the space where those images can meet.

## The Voice

Each reflection is spoken through the voice of a fictional elder who has become fluent in both spiritual languages.

He does not argue that Daoism and Christianity are identical. Nor does he attempt to translate every Daoist idea into Christian theology.

Instead, he listens.

For him, the bamboo forest and Galilee have become part of one contemplative landscape. A mountain stream may lead naturally to the waters of baptism. An empty bowl may sit beside broken bread. The Sage may teach something that brings to mind the Carpenter.

The connections are allowed to arise without being forced.

## Structure

The book follows all **81 chapters of the *Dao De Jing***.

Each chapter contains:

* A title inspired by the central image or theme
* Two contemplative reflections
* Daoist imagery woven naturally with Christian mystical reflection
* A short breath prayer for meditation

The entries are intended to be read slowly rather than consumed quickly. A single reflection may be enough for one morning or evening.

## Themes

Throughout the book, several themes continually return:

**Water** — softness that gradually overcomes hardness.

**Wu wei** — acting without forcing and learning to move with the grain of life.

**Yielding** — discovering strength that does not depend upon domination.

**Simplicity** — recognizing when enough is already enough.

**Humility** — becoming like the sea by taking the lowest place.

**Non-grasping** — doing the work and releasing the outcome.

**Compassion** — refusing to allow another person's darkness to create darkness within us.

**Contentment** — becoming present to the ordinary life already here.

**Mystery** — allowing the deepest things to remain larger than our explanations.

**Return** — discovering that the spiritual journey may ultimately be a return to what has always been nearest.

## A Christian Encounter with the Dao

This book is written from the perspective of a follower of Jesus who also regards Laozi as a profound teacher of wisdom.

It does not claim that the Dao and the Christian understanding of God are simply interchangeable concepts. The *Dao De Jing* emerged from ancient Chinese culture, while Jesus emerged from the Jewish spiritual world of the first century. Each deserves to be encountered within its own landscape.

Yet wisdom need not remain confined by cultural borders.

Water still flows downhill.

Seeds still disappear into darkness before rising.

Children still receive life before understanding it.

The strongest trees still know how to bend.

And human beings everywhere have wrestled with desire, fear, control, suffering, ambition, mortality, and the longing to live in harmony with something larger than themselves.

This devotional listens for those resonances.

## How to Read

There is no need to hurry.

Read one chapter.

Sit with the image.

Notice what stirs.

Allow the breath prayer to become quiet within you.

Then put the book down.

The purpose of these reflections is not primarily to provide more ideas about the spiritual life. It is to create a little space in which the reader might experience life differently.

Perhaps more gently.

Perhaps with less grasping.

Perhaps with greater trust.

## The Invitation

At the heart of this project is a simple invitation:

Be like water.

When life becomes hard, remain soft.

When the path changes, learn to yield.

When something beautiful enters your hands, receive it without possessing it.

When you have enough, let what overflows move toward another.

When you cannot control what comes next, attend faithfully to what is here.

And when you lose the Way, begin again beneath your own feet.

Perhaps our place within the Dao—within God's creation—is not something we must struggle to achieve.

Perhaps it is something we slowly remember when our striving becomes quiet.

The river is already flowing.

The invitation is simply to enter it.
 of the Dao De Jing
